# Questions may follow the maintainers' goals; answers stay neutral; advocacy lives outside the archive

Status: accepted (2026-11-23)

## Context
The maintainers are public school parents who also use this archive to build arguments elsewhere. The neutrality
rules in `RESEARCH.md` didn't say how that fits: a session could read them as ruling out questions that serve an
argument, or let the goal slant the answers. Claims against more school funding ("spending rose,
results fell") reach legislators whether or not we check them.

## Decision
Research questions may come from the maintainers' goals. Answers follow the same evidence rules whatever they show,
and the strongest opposing claims are researched as questions. The maintainers' own advocacy content lives outside
`sources/` and `analysis/` and cites into the archive. The rules are in `RESEARCH.md` ("Who it's for", "Neutral by
design"). The maintainers' specific goals are kept out of the repo.

## Options weighed
- **Say nothing** (the status quo): the goal shapes the questions anyway, unstated, and sessions can't tell an
  allowed question from bias.
- **An advocacy site**: one place for everything, but every figure reads as campaign material, and the archive
  loses its value with skeptical readers and legislators.
- **Neutral method, advocacy outside** (chosen): questions can serve the goal, answers stay checkable. Cost: the
  advocacy content needs a second home.
- **Pro and con side by side on every page**: looks balanced, but gives weak claims equal weight and doubles the work.
- **State the maintainers' specific goals in the repo**: most transparent, but hands critics an easy dismissal for
  little gain; the About page already says who runs the site, and whether to say more is a separate choice.

## Revisit when
Advocacy content needs to live on the same site, or a review finds the questions consistently skip evidence that
cuts the other way.
