# Briefing: Oregon's 2027-29 K-12 funding decision

Prepared 2026-11-20 from this archive. Citation formats follow `CLAUDE.md`. Documents and on-the-record statements
are cited; passages labeled Analysis are inference or arithmetic. Advocacy groups' claims are labeled as theirs. Open research
is tracked as GitHub issues, shown as (#16) and so on.

---

## (a) Bottom line

1. **What's being decided.** The Governor's Recommended Budget for 2027-29 is due Dec 1
   [SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html], built on the Nov 18 revenue forecast [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.33].
   The 2027 Legislature then sets the State School Fund (SSF), and may also change the QEM or the funding model
   [NEWS:NEWS-20260617-occ-1].
2. **The state's own benchmarks.** The 2026 Quality Education Model (QEM) report puts the SSF current service level
   (CSL) at $12.56B, the cost of keeping today's services at $13.78B, and the cost of meeting the state's goals at
   $16.21B [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
   With the SSF at CSL, about $1.2B remains to reach the status-quo total core funding for K-12
   [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103], and is $3.65B below the full QEM [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
3. **Why it's a state decision.** After Measures 5 (1990) and 50 (1997) capped property taxes, the state became the
   main source of school operating money [SRC:reference/oregon-encyclopedia_measure-5.html]. Districts can add a local
   option levy, but only a capped amount of it stays outside the equalization formula
   [SRC:legislature/olis/2018R1/146437_lro-school-local-option.pdf p.4] (section c).
4. **No funding commitment yet.** Gov. Kotek has set outcome goals (instructional time, SB 141 targets) and an
   executive order on instructional time, but has not said how she'd pay for her school goals
   [NEWS:NEWS-20260917-opb-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1]. She has said her budget will prioritize education
   [NEWS:NEWS-2026-salemreporter-1].
5. **Reserves are the other live question.** The Education Stability Fund is projected at $1,271M at the end of
   2025-27 [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41]. The Governor and the Ways & Means co-chairs have resisted
   using one-time money for ongoing costs [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1].

## (c) How we got here

**Measures 5 and 50 moved school funding to the state (#16)**
- **Measure 5 (1990)** capped school property taxes, phasing the limit down to $5 per $1,000 of real market value by
  1995-96 ($10 for other local government) [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.3]. Fully
  in effect, it cut tax rates an average of 51% from 1990-91 levels
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.2].
- Measure 5 required the state to replace schools' lost property tax through 1996, and Measure 50 added a
  constitutional requirement to replace school revenue lost to the Measure 5 limits. DOR says both had little effect
  on actual state school funding, because the Legislature appropriated more than required
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]. The state's share of K-12 operating
  money went from 28.6% in 1990-91 to 70.6% in 1998-99 [SRC:reference/oregon-encyclopedia_measure-5.html] (reference
  work; primary series still to find).
- **Measure 50 (1997)**:
  - rolled each property's assessed value back to 90% of its 1995-96 value;
  - capped growth in assessed value at 3% a year;
  - replaced most levies with permanent rates
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.3].
- **Local option levies** are the only way to raise operating money above the permanent rate. Schools could use them
  from 2000-01. Measure 50 required at least 50% of registered voters to vote in elections other than general
  elections [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]; Measure 56 (2008) later changed this
  for some elections (to confirm: #16).
- **The local option cap.** The amount kept outside the equalization formula is capped at the lesser of 20% of
  formula revenue or an indexed per-student amount ($1,343.92 in 2017-18)
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2018R1/146437_lro-school-local-option.pdf p.4]. 20 districts levied one in 2016-17
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2018R1/146437_lro-school-local-option.pdf p.5].
- Analysis: school operating budgets now depend mainly on the state General Fund and on decisions in Salem; a
  district can add only a capped levy. (Current cap, compression losses and the turnout rule today: #16.)

**The 2025-27 budget and the 2026 session**
- The Legislature adopted an $11.4B SSF for 2025-27 [SRC:legislature/press/2025-05-28_record-k12-funding.pdf p.1].
  Kotek had proposed $11.36B [NEWS:NEWS-20241203-osba-1].
- The Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation's Nov 2025 report says 2025-27 funding "effectively closes the
  funding gap and allows for the full implementation of the QEM" [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.4].
  The same report criticizes how the QEM is built [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.5].
- A JPEA legislative concept would move the cost model from the Quality Education Commission to the Legislative
  Policy and Research Office [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/311607_jpea-legislative-concepts.pdf p.1]. It became
  SB 1555 (2026), described by Rep. Ruiz as replacing the QEM [NEWS:NEWS-20260121-katu-1]. The overhaul effort led by
  Ruiz and Sen. Sollman was unsuccessful [NEWS:NEWS-20260617-occ-1].

**New requirements in 2026**
- Executive Order 26-06: no cuts to instructional time; districts restore 2024-25 levels by 2027-28
  [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_preserving-instructional-time.pdf p.2]. Its FAQ says funding options will be explored
  "within the existing budget" [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2].
- SB 141 statewide performance growth targets for the common metrics, adopted by the State Board on Apr 28, 2026,
  for 2029-30, e.g. 8th-grade math from 28.9% to 52.0%
  [SRC:ode/accountability/2026-05_statewide-performance-growth-targets.pdf p.1]. Fielding questions about funding, ODE
  Director Charlene Williams said meeting the targets "does not require new dollars", framing the work as districts
  using or redirecting the resources they have; a board member and a board adviser voiced doubts about meeting them
  without more money, and the board then voted unanimously [NEWS:NEWS-20260429-oregonian-1]. The Quality Education
  Commission's 2026 report offers, as an alternative if the full QEM isn't funded, more funding for programs aimed at
  the SB 141 metrics [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.110].

**The 2026 QEM and district pressure**
- The 2026 QEM report sets the 2027-29 benchmarks in section (d) [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103].
- PPS faces a $65M deficit. Its Board asks the Legislature to at least fund the CSL, and
  says that still leaves a gap [PPSARCH:BB:14673756 p.1].
- Other districts' cuts are not yet archived (#23).

## (f) Proposals and requests on the table

**On the amount**
- PPS Board: fund at least the CSL [PPSARCH:BB:14673756 p.1].
- Other groups' positions (Oregon Education Association, OSBA, COSA) are still to archive (#26).

**On the model**
- **Replacing the QEM** with a different cost model [NEWS:NEWS-20260121-katu-1] (#25). Rep. Ruiz's Nov 2026 post
  describes a new funding system and a cost model with more prototype school models [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].

**On reserves (#22)**
- **The rules.** Withdrawal from the Education Stability Fund takes a three-fifths vote in each chamber plus either an
  economic trigger or a governor's emergency declaration [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1].
- **PPS:** $500M to buy down PERS rates statewide [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]. Its chief financial officer said this would cut the PPS gap
  to about $42.7M [NEWS:NEWS-20261116-wweek-1].
- **Rep. Ricki Ruiz:** an initial proposal for one-time, targeted PERS relief to districts under financial strain,
  especially those that have already used their own reserves; no amount named [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].
- **Rep. Tammy Carpenter** suggested the fund as a bridge [NEWS:NEWS-20261112-katu-2].

## (g) How the money is shared: the formula

The formula in ORS chapter 327 divides the SSF among districts [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. These parts of it
are still to research.

- **Special education cap.** The formula adds weight for students on an IEP only up to 11% of a district's enrollment
  without state approval [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. HB 2953 (2025) as introduced would remove the cap
  [SRC:legislature/bills/2025R1_HB2953_introduced.pdf p.1]; its final status is still to confirm (#17).
- **Transportation.** The state reimburses an approved share of district transportation costs
  [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]; how closures change those costs is unresearched (#18).
- **English learner and poverty weights** (#19).
- **Online charters** (#20). The 20 virtual charters in ODE's 2025-26 report had about 22,100 ADMr (our sum
  [DER:virtual_charters/charter_count_2025_26] [DER:virtual_charters/admr_total_2025_26] from
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.2]). That is about 4% of statewide
  ADMr (our sum [DER:virtual_charters/admr_share_2025_26], using [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_ssf-estimates_2026-05-01.pdf p.1]).
  District sponsors must pass through at least 80% (K-8) or 95% (9-12) of their General Purpose Grant per ADMw
  [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html]; what they keep depends on each contract. See `topics/virtual-charters.md`.
- **Unfunded mandates**: requirements adopted without new money (section c) (#21).

## (h) What it buys: results and accountability

The archive so far covers mostly how much is spent. What the money buys is the least-researched part.

- **What the state now measures.** SB 141 statewide performance growth targets set goals for 2029-30
  [SRC:ode/accountability/2026-05_statewide-performance-growth-targets.pdf p.1]. ODE Director Charlene Williams
  framed meeting them as districts using or redirecting the resources they have; a board member and a board adviser
  doubted that would be enough [NEWS:NEWS-20260429-oregonian-1] (section c). Under SB 141, state help and oversight
  step up for districts that show no progress [NEWS:NEWS-20260429-oregonian-1]; the details, and ODE's Dec 2025
  progress report, are still to work through (#60).
- **What full funding is meant to buy.** If the full QEM isn't funded, the Quality Education Commission's 2026 report
  offers more funding for programs aimed at the SB 141 metrics [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.110]. The model
  assumes that sustained full funding, with districts using the recommended practices, would bring the four-year
  graduation rate to 90 percent [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.92]; outcomes inform the commission's judgment but
  are not inputs to the model [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.95]. See `topics/qem-practices-outcomes.md`.
- **What past increases achieved.** Student Investment Account spending and results (#57), and statewide outcomes over
  time set against funding (#58), are not yet archived.

## (i) Contested claims and open questions

- **Record increases vs real cuts.** 2025-27 was a record SSF [SRC:legislature/press/2025-05-28_record-k12-funding.pdf p.1],
  and the JPEA called the gap closed for 2025-27 [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.4]. The 2026 QEM
  sets a higher bar for 2027-29 [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103].
  - Analysis: both can be true; they measure different biennia against different models.
- **Is the QEM the right yardstick?** The JPEA report criticizes its method
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.5], and some legislators want to replace it
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260121-katu-1].
- **Per-student spending.** A claim that Oregon spends above the national average per student is unsourced here. The
  QEM report ranks Oregon 20th in state-sourced per-pupil funding (2024 Census), rising to 12th at full QEM
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
  - Analysis: total vs state-sourced spending are different measures.
- **The forecast.** The November gain is modest, and economists warned it may not last [NEWS:NEWS-20261118-klcc-1].
  The exact figure ($245M per news; DAS's release reportedly says $233M) is still to confirm (`open_questions.md`).
- **One-time money.** Lieber, Sanchez and Kotek object to using reserves for ongoing costs
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1]. Rep. Carpenter has suggested it as a bridge
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261112-katu-2], and Rep. Ruiz has floated one-time, targeted PERS relief [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].
  - Analysis: PERS contributions are an ongoing cost, so the objection may still apply to his version.
- **Unfunded mandates.** The EO 26-06 FAQ answers "No." to whether the order is an unfunded mandate
  [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.3], while saying funding may not be sufficient for some districts
  [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2].
