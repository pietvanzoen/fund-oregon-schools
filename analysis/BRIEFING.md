# Briefing: fixing how Oregon funds K-12 schools

Last updated 2026-11-20. For campaign volunteers and their Claude setups: this file sets the research direction
and feeds the parent talking points. Funding figures cite the 2026 QEM report; other claims still cite news
coverage until their primary documents are archived.

**The campaign:** statewide, led mostly by Portland parents, because more districts are weighing closures and
downsizing. **The goal:** fix how Oregon funds its schools. Two phases:
1. **Now to Dec 1:** press Gov. Kotek to put new money for K-12 in her 2027-29 recommended budget.
2. **The 2027 Legislature:** push the budget writers and education committees on the amount *and* on how the
   formula divides it.

Open research is tracked as GitHub issues; numbers below like (#16) point to them.

## Bottom line
- Gov. Kotek has made big promises about school **outcomes**, but no pledge to "fully fund" schools
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260917-opb-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
- The state's own commission says keeping schools at today's service level in 2027-29 takes **$13.78B** in state
  money, and the State School Fund's current service level covers only $12.56B of it, leaving **about $1.2B short
  before any improvement** [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103].
- Meeting Oregon's education goals takes **$16.21B**: $3.65B more than the current service level
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
- Since Measures 5 and 50, school operating money comes mostly from the state, so these are the Governor's and
  the Legislature's decisions; districts can't make up the difference locally (see "Why it's the state's job").
- Her budget is due Dec 1 [SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html]; she has said education will be a priority
  [NEWS:NEWS-2026-salemreporter-1].

## Why it's the state's job: Measures 5 and 50
- **Measure 5 (1990)** capped property taxes for schools, phasing the limit down to $5 per $1,000 of real market
  value by 1995-96 ($10 for other local government) [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.3].
  Fully in effect, it cut tax rates an average of 51% from 1990-91 levels
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.2].
- The state had to replace schools' lost property tax only through 1996, but the Legislature kept appropriating more
  than required [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]. The state's share of K-12
  operating money went from 28.6% in 1990-91 to 70.6% in 1998-99
  [SRC:reference/oregon-encyclopedia_measure-5.html] (reference work; primary series to find, #16).
- **Measure 50 (1997)** rolled each property's assessed value back to 90% of its 1995-96 value, capped growth in
  assessed value at 3% a year, and replaced most levies with permanent tax rates
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.3].
- **Local option levies** are the only way a district can raise operating money above its permanent rate. Schools
  could use them from 2000-01, and outside general elections they need at least 50% of registered voters to vote
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]. The amount kept outside the equalization
  formula is capped at the lesser of 20% of formula revenue or an indexed per-student amount ($1,343.92 in
  2017-18); 20 districts levied one in 2016-17 [SRC:legislature/olis/2018R1/146437_lro-school-local-option.pdf p.4]
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2018R1/146437_lro-school-local-option.pdf p.5].

Analysis: Measures 5 and 50 moved control of school budgets from local voters to Salem, and tied them to the state
General Fund, which runs mostly on the income tax. A district that's short can't fix it locally beyond a capped
levy. That's why a closure in Portland, Salem or a rural district is a state budget decision, and why the campaign
targets the Governor and the Legislature. (Current levy cap, compression losses and the 50% turnout rule after
Measure 56 still to confirm: #16.)

## Phase 1: the Governor's budget (now to Dec 1)

### The ask
> Governor Kotek, you set bold goals for Oregon's kids. Please fund them: dedicate new state revenue to K-12
> schools in your Dec 1 budget, above current service level.

Analysis: asking her to keep a funding pledge she never made invites a "that's not what I said" response.
Asking her to fund goals she did set (instructional time, attendance, SB 141 targets) uses her own words.

**Undecided: whether to name a figure** (#26). The two benchmarks are the ~$1.2B status-quo gap and the $3.65B
full-QEM gap above the current service level [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]
[SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]. "Above current service level" alone would be met by a token increase.

## Phase 2: the 2027 Legislature
The amount is half the fight; the other half is how the formula divides it. Directions to research, each with an
issue:

- **Special education cap.** The formula adds weight for students on an IEP only up to 11% of a district's
  enrollment without state approval [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. Families United reports about 17% of PPS
  students have an IEP and that HB 2953 (2025), which would have removed the cap, died in committee
  [SRC:orgs/families-united/demands-funding.html] [SRC:legislature/bills/2025R1_HB2953_introduced.pdf]. Statewide
  issue; strong parent message (#17).
- **Transportation.** The state reimburses an approved share of district transportation costs
  [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]; Families United says closures lengthen routes and push the extra cost onto
  PPS [SRC:orgs/families-united/demands-funding.html] (#18).
- **English learner and poverty weights**, and whether they fit urban districts (#19).
- **Online charters.** State money that follows students to virtual charters (#20).
- **Unfunded mandates.** SB 141 targets and EO 26-06 add requirements without new money (see her words above);
  map each mandate to its funding, if any (#21).
- **Formula reform is already on the table.** Rep. Ruiz and Sen. Sollman pushed in 2026 to replace the QEM with an
  outcomes-based approach [NEWS:NEWS-20260121-katu-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20260617-occ-1]; Sen. Frederick expects
  significant QEM changes in 2027 [NEWS:NEWS-20260617-occ-1]. Kotek wants to revisit enrollment-based funding
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260917-opb-1] (#25).
- **Education Stability Fund as a bridge.** Projected at $1,271M at the end of 2025-27
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41]. Withdrawal takes a three-fifths vote in each chamber plus either an
  economic trigger or an emergency declared by the Governor [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1]. See
  "Reserves" below (#22).
- **Community schools.** What the state can fund to fill buildings with preschool, aftercare and family services
  instead of closing them (#24).

Analysis: the campaign leans on QEM figures. If the 2027 session rewrites or replaces the QEM, the argument has to
stand on what schools need (instructional time, special education, staffing) rather than on the model's name.

## Context: statewide, and Portland
- **Statewide:** still mostly unresearched. Inventory the districts closing schools, cutting days or laying off
  staff (Salem-Keizer, Beaverton, Hillsboro, rural districts) so this doesn't read as a Portland problem (#23).
- PPS faces a $65M deficit; its Board asks the Legislature to at least fully fund the CSL and says that still
  leaves the gap [PPSARCH:BB:14673756 p.1].
- PPS asked for $500M from the Education Stability Fund to buy down PERS statewide [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1];
  its CFO said that would cut the gap to about $42.7M [NEWS:NEWS-20261116-wweek-1].

## Reserves: Education Stability Fund
- Balance and rules as above [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41] [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1].
- Kotek opposes one-time money for ongoing costs [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1]; Ways & Means co-chairs Lieber and
  Sanchez said in May 2026 they saw no reason yet to tap the fund [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1].
- Families United asks for an emergency declaration and a one-time bridge to keep schools open through 2027-28
  while the formula is fixed [SRC:orgs/families-united/demands-esf.html].

Analysis: as a one-time bridge tied to formula reform, the fund answers the "one-time money" objection better than
as general support. It still needs an emergency she has resisted and a supermajority, so it stays secondary to the
budget ask in Phase 1 and becomes a live option in Phase 2.

## Other campaigns
**Families United for Public Schools** (Portland families) has four demands: pause PPS closures, release the
Education Stability Fund, fix the funding formula, and make every school a community school
[SRC:orgs/families-united/demands.html]. They're holding a rally at the Capitol on Dec 2, the day after the
budget [SRC:orgs/families-united/demands.html].

This campaign is separate. Their formula, reserve and community-school demands overlap with Phase 2 and are
tracked above. The closure pause is a PPS Board decision and out of scope here. Their claims are theirs: check
them against primary sources before reuse. Their funding page compares the QEM figures with the 2025-27 budget,
not the CSL [SRC:orgs/families-united/demands-funding.html].

## Weak points to prepare for
- She will point to record SSF increases on her watch [NEWS:NEWS-20241203-osba-1]. Analysis: answer with the
  commission's own figure that the CSL is ~$1.2B below status quo [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103], and
  with her own mandates that came without money.
- The November forecast gain is modest and economists warned it may not last [NEWS:NEWS-20261118-klcc-1].
- Opponents argue Oregon already spends above the national average per student (source to add). The commission
  answers part of this: Oregon ranked 20th in state-sourced per-pupil funding (2024 Census) and would rise to 12th
  at the full QEM level [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]. Analysis: the two claims measure different things
  (total vs. state-sourced spending), so check the opponents' figure before rebutting it.
- The Legislature's own sufficiency report (Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation, Nov 2025) says
  2025-27 state funding "effectively closes the funding gap and allows for the full implementation of the QEM"
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.4]. Analysis: that compares 2025-27 funding to the 2024 QEM; the
  2026 QEM sets a new, higher bar for 2027-29, so a flat budget reopens the gap. Expect budget writers to cite it
  (see `people/ruiz.md`).
- Some legislators want to replace the QEM [NEWS:NEWS-20260121-katu-1]; expect "the model is flawed" in reply to
  QEM figures (#25).
- Ways & Means co-chairs Lieber and Sanchez said in May 2026 they saw no reason yet to tap the Education Stability
  Fund [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1]. See `people/lieber.md`, `people/sanchez.md`.
