# Briefing: funding Oregon K-12 in the 2027-29 budget

Last updated 2026-11-19. Seeded from the campaign's research brief of the same date. Many claims still cite news
coverage; replace those with primary documents as the queue is fetched (see `open_questions.md`).

## Bottom line
- Gov. Kotek has made big promises about school **outcomes**, but no pledge to "fully fund" schools
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260917-opb-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
- The state's own commission says schools need about **$2.5B more just to maintain current service** in 2027-29,
  and about **$5B more** to meet the new targets [NEWS:NEWS-20260812-opb-1] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf].
- Her budget comes out in December; she has said education will be a priority
  [NEWS:NEWS-2026-salemreporter-1].

## The ask
> Governor Kotek, you set bold goals for Oregon's kids. Please fund them: dedicate new state revenue to K-12
> schools in your Dec 1 budget, above current service level.

Analysis: asking her to keep a funding pledge she never made invites a "that's not what I said" response.
Asking her to fund goals she did set (instructional time, attendance, SB 141 targets) uses her own words.

## Context: Portland
- PPS faces a $65M deficit; its Board asks the Legislature to at least fully fund the CSL and says that still
  leaves the gap [PPSARCH:BB:14673756 p.1].
- PPS asked for $500M from the Education Stability Fund to buy down PERS statewide [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1];
  its CFO said that would cut the gap to about $42.7M [NEWS:NEWS-20261116-wweek-1].
- Analysis: the reserve request depends on an emergency declaration and a three-fifths vote
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1], and Kotek opposes one-time money for ongoing costs [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
  Keep it as supporting context; the budget ask is in her hands alone.

## Weak points to prepare for
- She will point to record SSF increases on her watch [NEWS:NEWS-20241203-osba-1].
- The November forecast gain is modest and economists warned it may not last [NEWS:NEWS-20261118-klcc-1].
- Opponents argue Oregon already spends above the national average per student (source to add).
