# What's being decided

These choices on Oregon K-12 funding will be made by a set date: the Governor's budget, the State School Fund amount,
whether to draw on the Education Stability Fund, the cost model and the funding formula. Each section gives the baseline and
options on the table, who stands where and when, and what the archive can answer so far. The tables are built from the archive's decision
files; the text is cited like any analysis. A due date for a decision by the Legislature is the latest the 2027
session can run. Background: `topics/measures-5-and-50.md` explains why these are state decisions.

**How to read the tables.** Every decision has a baseline; some also have benchmarks or proposals. These labels
are this site's own.

- **Baseline:** the point of comparison if nobody changes course. For the State School Fund it's the current service
  level, $12.56 billion, which covers the State School Fund only, not the state's other money for schools
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.100] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]. For the reserve fund, it's leaving
  the money in reserve.
- **Benchmark:** another figure to compare against, such as the 2025-27 amount or the Quality Education Commission's
  estimates of need.
- **Proposal:** what someone has proposed, who proposed it, when, and how formal it is.

<details class="info" markdown>
<summary>More on baselines, benchmarks and proposals</summary>

- Budget writers start from the current service level: the Department of Administrative Services' estimate for
  2027-29, made by adjusting the last budget's State School Fund spending for inflation and enrollment
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.100].
- The Quality Education Commission's report uses "baseline" differently, for its full model
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
- For the cost model and the formula, the baseline is keeping them as they are.
- The commission's estimates for 2027-29 are $13.78 billion to keep 2025-27 service levels and $16.21 billion to meet
  the state's goals [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]. Both count state money beyond the State School Fund, so compare them
  with each other, not with the current service level.
- A proposal's formality is one of: a law, a bill, a request, a platform, a statement or a social post.

</details>

Analysis: the choice of baseline matters. The commission counts about $1.2 billion of other state money on top of
the current service level for its status quo estimate [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]. How the model arrives
at that money is set out under the State School Fund decision below. Whether the current service level keeps today's
services is still open (`questions.csv`, "csl-meaning").

## The Governor's recommended budget for 2027-29 {#grb-2027-29}

- State law sets Dec. 1 of even-numbered years for a governor continuing in office to submit a budget
  [SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html]; Gov. Tina Kotek was reelected in November [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
- The budget will be based on the December 2026 revenue forecast, released Nov. 18, 2026
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.1] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.33]. That forecast
  raised its projection of net General Fund revenue for 2027-29 by $233.5 million over the previous forecast; with an
  $11.2 million larger beginning balance, projected available resources for 2027-29 rose $244.6 million
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.63]. That beginning balance is the current budget's projected ending balance
  on June 30, 2027, now $411 million [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.30].
- News reported about $245 million more for the upcoming budget cycle, about $233 million more for 2027-29 and about
  $11 million more for the current budget [NEWS:NEWS-20261118-klcc-1].
- Analysis: the reported $245 million matches the forecast's $244.6 million rise in available resources for 2027-29;
  the $11 million for the current budget reaches 2027-29 as the larger beginning balance, so it isn't new money on top
  of the $245 million [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.62] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.63].
- Each of the four statements on the forecast below gives its author's own reading of it. Kotek's, issued the day the
  forecast came out, said it "shows Oregon state government has steady financial footing on which to protect
  essential services". She added that "we must create more momentum to help families struggling to make ends meet.
  That means making responsible investments to lower costs, foster job growth, and create more economic opportunity
  across the state." The statement does not mention schools or reserves
  [SRC:gov/press/2026-11-18_kotek_revenue-forecast-statement.html].
- Three Republican statements the same day argued that much of the increase comes from
  investment income and stock market gains, and warned against basing the budget on those gains; Starr and Brock Smith warned
  against permanent spending in particular:
  - House Republican Leader Lucetta Elmer, R-District 24 [SRC:legislature/members/elmer.html], for the House
    Republican caucus: "We cannot build a responsible state budget around a strong stock market and hope it lasts"
    [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-18_house-republicans_revenue-forecast.pdf p.1].
  - Senate Republican Leader Bruce Starr, R-District 12 (Rural Polk & Yamhill Counties)
    [SRC:legislature/members/starr.html]: "The state can't keep making permanent spending decisions based on stock
    market gains that could disappear overnight."
    [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-18_senate-republicans_revenue-forecast.pdf p.1]
  - Sen. David Brock Smith, R-District 1 (Port Orford) [SRC:legislature/members/smithd.html]: "We cannot lock in
    permanent spending on temporary windfalls." He also called on Democrats to work with Republicans to "right-size
    government" [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11_brock-smith_revenue-forecast.pdf p.1].
- None of the four statements names a State School Fund amount or mentions the Education Stability Fund
  [SRC:gov/press/2026-11-18_kotek_revenue-forecast-statement.html]
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-18_house-republicans_revenue-forecast.pdf]
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-18_senate-republicans_revenue-forecast.pdf]
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11_brock-smith_revenue-forecast.pdf].
- The House Republican caucus's release and the Senate Republican Leader's release both point to the forecast's projection that K-12 enrollment
  will fall 13% over the next decade
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-18_house-republicans_revenue-forecast.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-18_senate-republicans_revenue-forecast.pdf p.1]. Elmer: "Thirteen percent fewer
  students in our schools over the next decade should get everyone's attention."
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-18_house-republicans_revenue-forecast.pdf p.1] Assuming enrollment keeps its
  current ratio to the school-age population, the forecast projects K-12 public school enrollment falling by 69,500
  students (12.9%) in the next 10 years, after a drop of 36,800 (6.4%) in 2015-25. It
  says the decline in the number of children "may indicate smaller budget allocation for the pre-K and K-12
  education", and that in the long run the state may face a labor shortage [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.48].
  How enrollment reaches the budget: the current service level adjusts the State School Fund total for projected
  enrollment [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.111], and the formula pays districts on the higher of the current or
  prior year's weighted enrollment, which the Quality Education Commission's report calls a "temporary financial
  glide path" for declining districts. That cushions a declining district's share, not the statewide total
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.114] [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html].
- On the points the statements make, the forecast itself says capital gains, dividend and retirement account income
  "continue to grow at double-digit rates as financial and equity markets soar to new heights", while the rest of
  income shows "historically normal gains" [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.6]. It warns that an equity
  correction like past ones "would impact Oregon’s revenue streams as well" [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.25].
  For the current budget, it puts the projected General Fund ending balance on June 30, 2027, at $411 million,
  "still $62 million less than what legislators left at the end of the 2025 session"
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.30].
- Analysis: the statements' revenue figures match the forecast. The $245 million in the House Republican and Brock
  Smith statements is the rise in available resources for 2027-29, and the $233 million in Starr's release is the rise
  in net General Fund revenue (above). The Republican statements argue against basing spending on these gains.
  Kotek's calls for "responsible investments" to lower costs, foster job growth and create economic opportunity,
  without tying them to the forecast's gains [SRC:gov/press/2026-11-18_kotek_revenue-forecast-statement.html]. The
  forecast reports fast growth in investment income and says a market correction would affect revenue; it makes no
  recommendation on spending [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf]. None of the statements says where the State School Fund should land against the current service level, so the archive
  records no position from them on the State School Fund amount (see the State School Fund decision below). The archive has no
  statement on this forecast from the House or Senate Democratic caucuses.
- Kotek has said her budget will prioritize education and summer learning [NEWS:NEWS-2026-salemreporter-1]. After the
  election, speaking about revenue proposals such as a wealth tax, she said she had made no commitments on how to pay
  for things [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
- Analysis: when the budget is released, compare its State School Fund amount with each figure in the comparison set
  for the next decision, not with one benchmark alone.

## The State School Fund amount for 2027-29 {#ssf-2027-29}

As of Nov. 22, 2026, the archive has no 2027-29 State School Fund amount from the Governor, legislative leaders or
the statewide education groups listed below. The Governor's recommended budget is due Dec. 1
[SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html]. The only proposal in the archive is from the Portland Public Schools Board of
Education. Its 2027 legislative agenda, presented at a Nov. 17 work session, asks the Legislature to fund at least the
current service level statewide, which the agenda credits to Gov. Tina Kotek and puts at $12.57 billion
[PPSARCH:BB:14673756 p.1]. The baseline on this page is the Department of Administrative Services' estimate in the
Quality Education Commission's report, $12.56 billion [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]; the archive has no
document from the Governor with the $12.57 billion figure.

- The Joint Committee on Ways and Means writes the budget; Sen. Kate
  Lieber, D-District 14 (Beaverton & Southwest Portland), returns as Senate co-chair
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-13_senate-dems-2027-leadership.pdf p.1] [SRC:legislature/members/lieber.html].
  Other 2027 roles aren't announced yet.
- The session begins Jan. 19, 2027 [SRC:legislature/ors/ors171.html] and can run to June 27
  [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html] [SRC:reference/2026-09-30_opdc-navigating-2027-session.pdf].
  Revenue forecasts are tentatively set for Feb. 24 and May 12 [SRC:fiscal/oea/2027_forecast-calendar.pdf].
- The current service level is $12.56 billion, the full Quality Education Model $16.21 billion, and the adopted
  2025-27 amount $11.36 billion [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]
  [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2025-07_lab-summary-2025-27.pdf p.1].
- The Quality Education Commission leaves the question of what is adequate, and which funding counts, to the
  Legislature [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
- The positions of the Oregon Education Association, the Oregon School Boards Association and the Coalition of Oregon
  School Administrators on the 2027-29 amount aren't archived yet.
- Analysis: the current service level is the State School Fund alone, while both Quality Education Model figures count
  other state money too, so the like-for-like gap between the two model scenarios is the status quo to the full
  model. See `topics/quality-education-model.md`.
- Analysis: by the Quality Education Model's statewide measure, a State School Fund at the current service level keeps
  the status quo (continuing 2025-27 activities) only if the state's grant programs outside the fund continue at about their
  2025-27 amounts and the model's status quo already prices what they pay for. The archive can't settle that second
  point yet; a question to Department of Education staff is drafted. If it fails, the $1.2 billion is left to other
  state money or to reduced services, and the difference between the two readings is roughly the size of the grants.
  The archive has no district-level estimate, and districts losing federal money can still have less to spend. See
  `topics/quality-education-model.md`.
- The model's status quo ($13.78 billion) is the current service level plus $1.22 billion of state money from outside
  the State School Fund; the table that derives it lists no state grant program among the revenue it subtracts
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]. The Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation counted $2.2 billion
  of state grants for core K-12 programs in 2025-27, led by $1.1 billion of Student Investment Account grants
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310959_jpea-adequacy-report.pdf p.29].
- The current service level updates the last budget for pay, benefits, pension rates, inflation and enrollment
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.111] [SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/307212_sb5516-ssf-work-session.pdf p.2], and
  leaves out declining federal money and falling earnings on districts' pension side accounts
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.100]. Whether the grant programs' own 2027-29 budgets carry them forward isn't
  archived yet.

## Drawing on the Education Stability Fund in 2027-29 {#esf-draw-2027-29}

- The fund, one of the state's two general reserve accounts, is projected at $1.271 billion at the end of 2025-27
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41].
- Withdrawal takes a three-fifths vote in each chamber plus either an economic trigger or a governor's emergency
  declaration [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1].
- Whether a trigger is met (question `esf-trigger-2027`), the revenue tests: the September 2025 forecast, released
  Aug. 27, 2025, put 2025-27 gross General Fund revenue $627.9 million below the close-of-session forecast the budget
  was built on; the December 2026 forecast puts it $205.8 million above [SRC:fiscal/oea/2025-09_forecast.pdf p.1] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2025-09_forecast.pdf p.27]
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.29]. Our estimate: the largest decline in any of the six 2025-27 forecasts was
  1.76%, in September 2025; the 2% test needs a decline of at least 2% [DER:reserves/esf_2pct_gross_max_decline].
  The 3% test asks whether the last forecast of the budget period puts General Fund money available for the next
  budget at least 3% below General Fund appropriations for the current budget period; that forecast is tentatively scheduled for May 12, 2027 [SRC:fiscal/oea/2027_forecast-calendar.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.33] [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. Our estimate
  from the December 2026 forecast: 2027-29 net General Fund revenue (after offsets and transfers) is about 13.5% more than
  2025-27 appropriations, leaving out the beginning balance [DER:reserves/esf_3pct_2026-12].
- The jobs test needs a decline in seasonally adjusted payroll jobs "for two or more consecutive quarters in the
  last 12 months" [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. The forecasters' first estimates showed
  declines in January-March 2026 (-0.8% at an annual rate) and April-June 2026 (-0.3%)
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-06_forecast.pdf p.48] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-09_forecast.pdf p.47]. The September 2026
  forecast revised January-March up, from 1,967.9 thousand jobs to 1,976.0 thousand
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-06_forecast.pdf p.50] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-09_forecast.pdf p.49], and gives no figure for
  October-December 2025, so the archive can't say whether January-March still shows a decline. No archived source
  from the fiscal offices, the forecasters or Legislative Counsel says whether a trigger is met now. The Oregon School
  Boards Association and the Coalition of Oregon School Administrators wrote in April 2026, before the June 2026
  forecast first showed the January-March decline, that the 2026 Legislature used neither the Education Stability
  Fund nor the Rainy Day Fund to balance the budget "despite meeting the economic triggers to access those funds", without saying which
  [SRC:orgs/osba-cosa/2026-04-08_legislative-report.pdf p.4].
- Analysis: so far no forecast has met a revenue test, so for now a draw turns on the jobs test or an emergency
  declaration. If the Legislature finds the jobs test met, a draw needs only the three-fifths votes; if not, it also
  needs Kotek's emergency declaration. Asked in April 2026 about a draw in the middle of the 2025-27 budget, her office
  said she did not then plan to declare an emergency and call a special session unless the presiding officers indicated three-fifths
  support [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2] [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.3]; the archive has no statement from her
  on a declaration in the 2027 session. The archive has no source on how the jobs test's 12 months are counted for a
  finding in 2027, or on whether a trigger met in 2025-27 supports spending in 2027-29; the 2021 dispute between
  Legislative Counsel and Gov. Kate Brown (below) was over a trigger met earlier in the budget period. For 2027-29
  itself, the 2% test can't be applied until the 2027-29 close-of-session forecast exists, after the 2027 session. See `topics/reserves.md`.
- State reserves are currently 9.7% [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast_slides.pdf p.37]; the projected 2027-29 balances
  ($2.9 billion in the Rainy Day Fund, $1.6 billion in the Education Stability Fund) would be 10.6% of the General Fund projection
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41].
- The Legislative Fiscal Office lists nine allocations from the fund, most recently $400 million approved in 2020 to rebalance the 2019-21 budget during
  the COVID-19 emergency [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3]
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2021R1/241974_sb226-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1]. Our sum of the nine allocations is $1,540.6 million,
  not adjusted for inflation [DER:reserves/esf_withdrawals_total].
- In 2021, a school funding plan from House and Senate Democrats relied on $200 million from the fund
  [SRC:legislature/press/2021-05-11_democrats-9.3b-school-funding-plan.pdf p.1]. Gov. Kate Brown vetoed the section of
  the State School Fund bill, Senate Bill 5514, that allowed spending it [SRC:legislature/laws/2021orlaw0289.pdf p.1],
  arguing that the conditions for using the fund, a downturn or another emergency, were not met
  [NEWS:NEWS-20210617-eastoregonian-1], while a Legislative Counsel opinion had argued that a trigger met earlier in
  the budget period left the fund available [NEWS:NEWS-20210512-opb-1]; lawmakers kept the State School Fund at $9.3 billion mainly with lottery money
  instead [SRC:fiscal/lro/2021-10_2021-measures-passed.pdf p.28]. The Legislative Fiscal Office's lottery tables show
  no withdrawal in 2021-23, 2023-25 or 2025-27, as of March 2026 [SRC:legislature/olis/2023R1/276164_hb5029-revenue-allocations.pdf p.3]
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/315811_sb5703-revenue-allocations.pdf p.3].
- Tammy Carpenter, D-District 27 (Beaverton and Cedar Hills), who won the seat in the Nov. 3, 2026, election
  (unofficial results), at a forum on Portland Public Schools' 2027-28 gap, suggested drawing on the fund to help schools while lawmakers
  work on a longer-term plan, with no amount [NEWS:NEWS-20261112-katu-2] [SRC:legislature/members/2026-11-03_general-election-results_state-representative.html]
  [SRC:legislature/members/helm.html]. The archive has no statement on the fund since May 2026 from Sen. Kate Lieber, D-District 14 (Beaverton &
  Southwest Portland) [SRC:legislature/members/lieber.html], or Rep. Tawna Sanchez, D-District 43 (North and
  Northeast Portland) [SRC:legislature/members/sanchez.html], the Ways and Means co-chairs in 2026
  [SRC:legislature/appointments/2026-01-23_house-committee-assignments.pdf p.3]
  [SRC:legislature/appointments/2026_senate-committee-assignments.pdf p.7].
- Rep. Ricki Ruiz, D-District 50 (Gresham), describes his proposal, one-time fund money for targeted relief on Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) costs, as
  immediate relief while longer-term reform is developed
  [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt] [SRC:legislature/members/ruiz.html].
- What the rules allow for PERS relief (question `esf-pers-relief`): withdrawals must go to public education, which the
  Legislative Fiscal Office describes as K-12 and post-secondary education, with some public early learning
  programs possibly eligible [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.2]. No archived source says whether paying down districts'
  pension costs counts, and none of the past withdrawals listed was for pension costs
  [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3]. On cost, Portland Public Schools asks for $500 million
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]. Our estimate: that is about 39% of the $1.271 billion balance projected for the end of
  2025-27 [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41] [DER:reserves/pps_ask_share_of_esf]. Ruiz names no amount and no test for which districts would qualify
  beyond "significant financial strain" [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt]. The
  archive has no statewide total of district budget gaps for 2027-28.
- Analysis: every option here needs the same vote and trigger. Lieber objects on the record to one-time money
  for ongoing costs [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1]; Kotek has described a similar objection, from unnamed legislative leaders, as
  one she can understand [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1], and has said tapping one-time reserves for Portland Public Schools'
  budget problems goes against enrollment-driven state investment [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1]. PERS
  contributions are an ongoing cost, so the objection could apply both to Ruiz's proposal and to Portland Public Schools' request for $500 million to
  buy down PERS rates [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1], while
  supporters describe a one-time draw as a bridge until lasting reform. The objection stated by Sanchez is mainly timing:
  saving the fund for worse years. Kotek's stance in April 2026 and Lieber's and Sanchez's in May 2026 were about a draw in 2025-27
  [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2] [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.3] [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1]; Kotek's
  November 2026 stance and Carpenter's suggestion came in the debate over Portland Public Schools' 2027-28 gap
  [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20261112-katu-2], and her October 2026 comments were reported in it
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]. In 2021 Gov. Kate Brown vetoed spending the Legislature had budgeted from the fund, arguing it
  didn't meet the conditions for using the fund [NEWS:NEWS-20210617-eastoregonian-1], so the Governor's reading of
  those conditions matters as well as the votes. See
  `topics/reserves.md`.

## Changing or replacing the Quality Education Model {#cost-model-2027}

- Senate Bill 1555 (2026), the Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation's bill to replace the Quality
  Education Model, did not pass: the commission's
  October 2026 materials list it as "Did not pass" [SRC:ode/qec/minutes/2026-10-21.pdf p.78]. The Senate Committee
  on Education met on it Feb. 10 and Feb. 12, 2026
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/313691_sb1555-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1], and it was still there when the 2026
  session's final business concluded March 6
  [SRC:ode/legislation/2026_summary-enacted-education-legislation.pdf p.4]. The Legislature's bill-tracking site
  still lists it in that committee, with no chapter number
  [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_overview.html]. A 2026 overhaul effort led by Ruiz and Sen. Janeen Sollman,
  D-District 15 (Forest Grove, Cornelius, Hillsboro, Rock Creek) [SRC:legislature/members/sollman.html], then a
  co-chair of the Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.3], was unsuccessful
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260617-occ-1].
- A legislative concept to move the cost model to the Legislative Policy and Research Office
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/311607_jpea-legislative-concepts.pdf p.1] became SB 1555 (2026), which would also
  have abolished the Quality Education Commission [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_overview.html].
- As introduced, SB 1555 would have priced new goals (every district meeting the requirements of a "standard"
  school district, licensed and qualified teachers, the targets set under Senate Bill 141), had a contractor of the Legislative Policy
  and Research Office build a new model at least every eight years, and had the Joint Interim Committee on Ways and
  Means judge sufficiency [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_introduced.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_introduced.pdf p.2] [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_introduced.pdf p.3]
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/313691_sb1555-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1].
- For the report published after Nov. 1, 2027 (the next one, after the 2027 session), the Department of
  Administrative Services would have adjusted the costs in the report adopted Nov. 19, 2025 for inflation and other
  costs, rather than use a new model, which was due by Feb. 1, 2028
  [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_introduced.pdf p.4]. That is the joint committee's adequacy report
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310959_jpea-adequacy-report.pdf p.1], which found 2025-27 funding "effectively closes
  the funding gap" [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310959_jpea-adequacy-report.pdf p.4].
- On record from the commission, in response to the outside evaluation: it is exploring more adequacy goals and
  agrees more prototype schools should be added, but has lacked the staff to do so
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.99]. Hearing testimony on SB 1555 isn't archived.
