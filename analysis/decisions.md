# What's being decided

These choices on Oregon K-12 funding will be made by a set date: the Governor's budget, the State School Fund amount,
whether to draw on the Education Stability Fund, the cost model and the funding formula. Each section gives the baseline and
options on the table, who stands where and when, and what the archive can answer so far. The tables are built from the archive's decision
files; the text is cited like any analysis. A due date for a decision by the Legislature is the latest the 2027
session can run. Background: `topics/measures-5-and-50.md` explains why these are state decisions.

**How to read the tables.** Every decision has a baseline; some also have benchmarks or proposals. These labels
are this site's own.

- **Baseline:** the point of comparison if nobody changes course. For the State School Fund it's the current service
  level, $12.56 billion, which covers the State School Fund only, not the state's other money for schools
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.100] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]. For the reserve fund, it's leaving
  the money in reserve.
- **Benchmark:** another figure to compare against, such as the 2025-27 amount or the Quality Education Commission's
  estimates of need.
- **Proposal:** what someone has proposed, who proposed it, when, and how formal it is.

<details class="info" markdown>
<summary>More on baselines, benchmarks and proposals</summary>

- Budget writers start from the current service level: the Department of Administrative Services' estimate for
  2027-29, made by adjusting the last budget's State School Fund spending for inflation and enrollment
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.100].
- The Quality Education Commission's report uses "baseline" differently, for its full model
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
- For the cost model and the formula, the baseline is keeping them as they are.
- The commission's estimates for 2027-29 are $13.78 billion to keep 2025-27 service levels and $16.21 billion to meet
  the state's goals [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]. Both count state money beyond the State School Fund, so compare them
  with each other, not with the current service level.
- A proposal's formality is one of: a law, a bill, a request, a platform, a statement or a social post.

</details>

Analysis: the choice of baseline matters. The commission counts about $1.2 billion of other state money on top of
the current service level for its status quo estimate [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]. How the model arrives
at that money is set out under the State School Fund decision below. Whether the current service level keeps today's
services is still open (`questions.csv`, "csl-meaning").

## The Governor's recommended budget for 2027-29 {#grb-2027-29}

- State law sets Dec. 1 of even-numbered years for a governor continuing in office to submit a budget
  [SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html]; Gov. Tina Kotek was reelected in November [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
- The budget will be based on the December 2026 revenue forecast, released Nov. 18, 2026
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.1] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.33]. That forecast
  raised its projection of net General Fund revenue for 2027-29 by $233.5 million over the previous forecast; with an
  $11.2 million larger beginning balance, projected available resources for 2027-29 rose $244.6 million
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.63]. That beginning balance is the current budget's projected ending balance
  on June 30, 2027, now $411 million [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.30].
- News reported about $245 million more for the upcoming budget cycle, about $233 million more for 2027-29 and about
  $11 million more for the current budget [NEWS:NEWS-20261118-klcc-1].
- Analysis: the reported $245 million matches the forecast's $244.6 million rise in available resources for 2027-29;
  the $11 million for the current budget reaches 2027-29 as the larger beginning balance, so it isn't new money on top
  of the $245 million [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.62] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.63].
- Kotek has said her budget will prioritize education and summer learning [NEWS:NEWS-2026-salemreporter-1]. After the
  election she declined to commit to how she would pay for her school goals [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
- Analysis: when the budget is released, compare its State School Fund amount with each figure in the comparison set
  for the next decision, not with one benchmark alone.

## The State School Fund amount for 2027-29 {#ssf-2027-29}

As of Nov. 22, 2026, the archive has no 2027-29 State School Fund amount from the Governor, legislative leaders or
the statewide education groups listed below. The Governor's recommended budget is due Dec. 1
[SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html]. The only proposal in the archive is from the Portland Public Schools Board of
Education. Its 2027 legislative agenda, presented at a Nov. 17 work session, asks the Legislature to fund at least the
current service level statewide, which the agenda credits to Gov. Tina Kotek and puts at $12.57 billion
[PPSARCH:BB:14673756 p.1]. The baseline on this page is the Department of Administrative Services' estimate in the
Quality Education Commission's report, $12.56 billion [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]; the archive has no
document from the Governor with the $12.57 billion figure.

- The Joint Committee on Ways and Means writes the budget; Sen. Kate
  Lieber, D-District 14 (Beaverton & Southwest Portland), returns as Senate co-chair
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-13_senate-dems-2027-leadership.pdf p.1] [SRC:legislature/members/lieber.html].
  Other 2027 roles aren't announced yet.
- The session begins Jan. 19, 2027 [SRC:legislature/ors/ors171.html] and can run to June 27
  [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html] [SRC:reference/2026-09-30_opdc-navigating-2027-session.pdf].
  Revenue forecasts are tentatively set for Feb. 24 and May 12 [SRC:fiscal/oea/2027_forecast-calendar.pdf].
- The current service level is $12.56 billion, the full Quality Education Model $16.21 billion, and the adopted
  2025-27 amount $11.36 billion [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]
  [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2025-07_lab-summary-2025-27.pdf p.1].
- The Quality Education Commission leaves the question of what is adequate, and which funding counts, to the
  Legislature [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
- The positions of the Oregon Education Association, the Oregon School Boards Association and the Coalition of Oregon
  School Administrators on the 2027-29 amount aren't archived yet (#26).
- Analysis: the current service level is the State School Fund alone, while both Quality Education Model figures count
  other state money too, so the like-for-like gap between the two model scenarios is the status quo to the full
  model. See `topics/quality-education-model.md`.
- Analysis: by the Quality Education Model's statewide measure, a State School Fund at the current service level keeps
  the status quo (today's services) only if the state's grant programs outside the fund continue at about their
  2025-27 amounts and the model's status quo already prices what they pay for. The archive can't settle that second
  point yet; a question to Department of Education staff is drafted (#146). If it fails, the $1.2 billion is left to other
  state money or to reduced services, and the difference between the two readings is roughly the size of the grants.
  The archive has no district-level estimate, and districts losing federal money can still have less to spend. See
  `topics/quality-education-model.md`.
- The model's status quo ($13.78 billion) is the current service level plus $1.22 billion of state money from outside
  the State School Fund; the table that derives it lists no state grant program among the revenue it subtracts
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103]. The Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation counted $2.2 billion
  of state grants for core K-12 programs in 2025-27, led by $1.1 billion of Student Investment Account grants
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310959_jpea-adequacy-report.pdf p.29].
- The current service level updates the last budget for pay, benefits, pension rates, inflation and enrollment
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.111] [SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/307212_sb5516-ssf-work-session.pdf p.2], and
  leaves out declining federal money and falling earnings on districts' pension side accounts
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.100]. Whether the grant programs' own 2027-29 budgets carry them forward isn't
  archived yet (#136).

## Drawing on the Education Stability Fund in 2027-29 {#esf-draw-2027-29}

- The fund, one of the state's two general reserve accounts, is projected at $1.271 billion at the end of 2025-27
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41].
- Withdrawal takes a three-fifths vote in each chamber plus either an economic trigger or a governor's emergency
  declaration [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1].
- State reserves are currently 9.7% [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast_slides.pdf p.37]; the projected 2027-29 balances
  ($2.9 billion in the Rainy Day Fund, $1.6 billion in the Education Stability Fund) would be 10.6% of the General Fund projection
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41].
- In 2021, a school funding plan from House and Senate Democrats relied on $200 million from the fund
  [SRC:legislature/press/2021-05-11_democrats-9.3b-school-funding-plan.pdf p.1]. The archive doesn't yet show whether
  it passed as proposed.
- Rep. Ricki Ruiz, D-District 50 (Gresham), describes his proposal, one-time fund money for targeted relief on Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) costs, as
  immediate relief while longer-term reform is developed
  [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt] [SRC:legislature/members/ruiz.html].
- Analysis: every option here needs the same vote and trigger. Kotek and Lieber object on the record to one-time money
  for ongoing costs. PERS contributions are an ongoing cost, so the objection could apply both to Ruiz's proposal and to Portland Public Schools' request for $500 million to
  buy down PERS rates [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1], while
  supporters describe a one-time draw as a bridge until lasting reform. The objection stated by Rep. Tawna
  Sanchez, D-District 43 (North and Northeast Portland) [SRC:legislature/members/sanchez.html], is mainly timing:
  saving the fund for worse years. The stances recorded so far were about a draw in 2025-27, not 2027-29. See
  `topics/reserves.md`.

## Changing or replacing the Quality Education Model {#cost-model-2027}

- Senate Bill 1555 (2026), the Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation's bill to replace the Quality
  Education Model, did not pass: the commission's
  October 2026 materials list it as "Did not pass" [SRC:ode/qec/minutes/2026-10-21.pdf p.78]. The Senate Committee
  on Education met on it Feb. 10 and Feb. 12, 2026
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/313691_sb1555-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1], and it was still there when the 2026
  session's final business concluded March 6
  [SRC:ode/legislation/2026_summary-enacted-education-legislation.pdf p.4]. The Legislature's bill-tracking site
  still lists it in that committee, with no chapter number
  [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_overview.html]. A 2026 overhaul effort led by Ruiz and Sen. Janeen Sollman,
  D-District 15 (Forest Grove, Cornelius, Hillsboro, Rock Creek) [SRC:legislature/members/sollman.html], then a
  co-chair of the Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.3], was unsuccessful
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260617-occ-1].
- A legislative concept to move the cost model to the Legislative Policy and Research Office
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/311607_jpea-legislative-concepts.pdf p.1] became SB 1555 (2026), which would also
  have abolished the Quality Education Commission [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_overview.html].
- As introduced, SB 1555 would have priced new goals (every district meeting the requirements of a "standard"
  school district, licensed and qualified teachers, the targets set under Senate Bill 141), had a contractor of the Legislative Policy
  and Research Office build a new model at least every eight years, and had the Joint Interim Committee on Ways and
  Means judge sufficiency [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_introduced.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_introduced.pdf p.2] [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_introduced.pdf p.3]
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/313691_sb1555-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1].
- For the report published after Nov. 1, 2027 (the next one, after the 2027 session), the Department of
  Administrative Services would have adjusted the costs in the report adopted Nov. 19, 2025 for inflation and other
  costs, rather than use a new model, which was due by Feb. 1, 2028
  [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_introduced.pdf p.4]. That is the joint committee's adequacy report
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310959_jpea-adequacy-report.pdf p.1], which found 2025-27 funding "effectively closes
  the funding gap" [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310959_jpea-adequacy-report.pdf p.4].
- On record from the commission, in response to the outside evaluation: it is exploring more adequacy goals and
  agrees more prototype schools should be added, but has lacked the staff to do so
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.99]. Hearing testimony on SB 1555 isn't archived.
- Analysis: the report published after Nov. 1, 2027 would follow the 2027 session, so it would be the one on 2027-29
  funding. A new cost model would also change the benchmarks the other decisions are measured against: the full-model
  and status quo figures are Quality Education Model scenarios, while the current service level and adopted amounts are
  not. A replacement built on SB 1555's goals would price a different target, so its figure would not be directly
  comparable with the Quality Education Model's. See `topics/quality-education-model.md` (SB 1555 section), and
  `topics/qem-practices-outcomes.md` for what the model assumes full funding buys and the 2025 outside evaluation of
  it.

## Changes to the funding formula {#formula-2027}

- Analysis: as of Nov. 22, 2026, the archive has no formula proposal from Kotek, and a web search found no
  2027 committee assignment memos, so who will hear formula bills in 2027 isn't settled (#107).
- The formula's weights and caps are set in chapter 327 of the Oregon Revised Statutes [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. See
  `topics/formula.md`.
- Kotek, Nov. 6, 2026, as quoted by KATU: "Maybe enrollment is the wrong marker. That was established almost 30 years
  ago. Maybe it has to be student need." She called rethinking it "a longer conversation than what is happening in
  districts right now" [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1].
- House Democrats chose Rep. Ben Bowman, D-District 25, as their nominee for speaker on Nov. 20, 2026; the full House
  votes in January [NEWS:NEWS-20261120-opb-1] [NEWS:NEWS-20261120-occ-1] [SRC:legislature/members/bowman.html]. The speaker appoints House committee chairs
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261120-occ-1]. The 2026 chairs are in `topics/formula.md`.
