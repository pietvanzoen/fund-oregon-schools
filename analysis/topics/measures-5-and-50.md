# Measures 5 and 50: why school funding is a state decision

Decisions this informs: `decisions.md` (all). Open research: #16, #65, #66, #67.

## Analysis

Measure 5 (1990) capped school property tax rates and Measure 50 (1997) capped growth in assessed value (sources
below), so school operating budgets now depend mainly on the state General Fund and on decisions in Salem; a district can add
only a capped local option levy. That is why the decisions in `decisions.md` are made by the governor and the Legislature.

Still to research: today's levy cap, "compression losses" (the gap between what
districts' operating taxes, not just levies, would raise and what the Measure 5 limits let them collect; bonds are
exempt) [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.7],
whether Measure 56 (2008) changed Measure 50's turnout rule (at least half of registered voters must vote, outside
general elections) for some elections, and the kicker, the law that returns income tax surpluses to taxpayers
[SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.1] (#66, #67).

## What the sources say

- **Measure 5 (1990)** capped school property taxes, phasing the limit down to $5 per $1,000 of real market value by
  1995-96 ($10 for other local government) [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.3].
- Fully in effect, Measure 5 cut tax rates an average of 51% from 1990-91 levels
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.2].
- Measure 5 required the state to replace schools' lost property tax through 1996, and Measure 50 added a
  constitutional requirement to replace school revenue lost to the Measure 5 limits. The Department of Revenue says
  both had little effect on actual state school funding, because the Legislature appropriated more than required
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4].
- The state's share of K-12 operating money went from 28.6% in 1990-91 to 70.6% in 1998-99
  [SRC:reference/oregon-encyclopedia_measure-5.html] (a reference work; the primary state series is still to find).
- **Measure 50 (1997)** rolled each property's assessed value back to 90% of its 1995-96 value, capped growth in
  assessed value at 3% a year, and replaced most levies with permanent rates
  [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.3].
- **Local option levies** are the only way to raise operating money above the permanent rate. Schools could use them
  from 2000-01. Measure 50 required at least 50% of registered voters to vote in elections other than general
  elections [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4].
- **The local option cap.** The amount kept outside the equalization formula is capped at the lesser of 20% of
  formula revenue or an indexed per-student amount ($1,343.92 in 2017-18)
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2018R1/146437_lro-school-local-option.pdf p.4]; collections over the cap go into the pool of
  formula revenue [SRC:legislature/olis/2018R1/146437_lro-school-local-option.pdf p.3]. Twenty districts levied one in 2016-17
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2018R1/146437_lro-school-local-option.pdf p.5].
