# The Education Stability Fund: Oregon's school reserve

## Analysis

Any draw from the Education Stability Fund needs a three-fifths vote in each chamber and either an economic trigger or an emergency
declaration. For a
2025-27 draw, Gov. Tina Kotek said she did not plan at that time to declare an emergency unless the votes were there. Revenue the
forecast already expects needs neither, though a tax increase also needs three-fifths of each chamber
[SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. That holds for a large, general draw. An initial proposal (November 2026) for
targeted, one-time Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) relief from Rep. Ricki Ruiz, D-District 50 (Gresham), a
co-chair of the Ways and Means Education Subcommittee in 2026, suggests a narrower
draw is being discussed, though it rests on one social media post with no amount or bill. It still needs the same
vote and trigger. The objection on record from Sen. Kate Lieber, D-District 14 (Beaverton & Southwest
Portland) [SRC:legislature/members/lieber.html], to one-time money for ongoing costs could still apply: PERS
contributions are an ongoing cost. Gov. Tina Kotek has said tapping one-time reserves for Portland Public Schools'
budget problems goes against enrollment-driven state investment. On one-time money for ongoing costs, she has
described a similar objection, from unnamed legislative leaders who dismissed using the fund when she raised it, as
one she can understand, framing it as their argument (below).
The objection stated by Rep. Tawna Sanchez, D-District 43 (North and Northeast Portland)
[SRC:legislature/members/sanchez.html], is mainly timing. Ruiz describes his proposal as relief
while longer-term reform is developed (`decisions.md#esf-draw-2027-29`).

No money has been taken from the fund since the 2020 transfer, and none of its past uses paid for pension costs (below). The
nine allocations the Legislative Fiscal Office lists, $1,540.6 million in all, not adjusted for inflation
[DER:reserves/esf_withdrawals_total], went to the State School Fund or other education programs, mostly to balance or
rebalance the state budget in the downturns of 2001-03, 2007-11 and 2020. The last draw the Legislature wrote into a
budget bill, $200 million for the 2021-23 State School Fund, did not take effect: Gov. Kate Brown vetoed it, arguing
that the conditions for using the fund's principal, a downturn or another emergency affecting school budgets, were
not met, while a Legislative Counsel opinion had argued that a trigger met earlier in the budget period left the fund
available. Lawmakers kept the State School Fund at $9.3 billion mainly with lottery money instead. The Legislative
Fiscal Office's lottery tables show no withdrawal in 2021-23, 2023-25 or 2025-27, as of March 2026. If the
Legislature draws on the fund in 2027-29, then it would be the first withdrawal since 2020 and the first in the record
for pension costs; the 2021 veto shows that a governor's reading of the conditions can stop spending the
Legislature has budgeted from the fund.

What a one-time draw for districts' budget gaps would cost statewide isn't known. The archive has no statewide total
of district budget gaps for 2027-28; the only district figure is Portland Public Schools' own projection of a $65
million gap. The district's $500 million request for all districts' pension rates would be about 39% of the $1.271
billion balance projected for the end of 2025-27 in the December 2026 forecast [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41]
[DER:reserves/pps_ask_share_of_esf].

## What the sources say

- The Education Stability Fund, one of the state's two general reserve accounts, is projected at $1.271 billion at
  the end of 2025-27 in the December 2026 revenue forecast, released Nov. 18, 2026
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.1] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41]. Withdrawal takes a
  three-fifths vote in each chamber plus either an economic trigger or a governor's emergency declaration
  [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1] (news coverage: [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]).
- Withdrawals must be used for public education; the fund has no repayment provision; its cap is 5% of General Fund
  revenue in the prior budget period [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1]. The Legislative Fiscal Office
  says public education here means K-12 and post-secondary education, and that some public early learning programs may
  be eligible [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.2].
  Unlike the Rainy Day Fund, the Education Stability Fund has no limit of two-thirds of its balance per budget period
  [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1].
- The fund held $1,012 million on July 1, 2025, and $1,041 million on Oct. 1, 2025
  [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1].

### Past allocations

- The Legislative Fiscal Office lists nine allocations from the fund through 2020 (amounts as recorded in the
  state budget system) [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3]:

  | Budget period | $ million | What it was for | Source |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2001-03 | 150.0 | Initial transfer to the State School Fund in the constitutional amendment that created the fund, freeing General Fund for other purposes | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |
  | 2001-03 | 107.4 | Part of the final 2001-03 budget rebalance, 2003 session | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |
  | 2003-05 | 126.5 | The 2003 Legislature sent 90% of the lottery money due to the fund directly to the State School Fund | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |
  | 2007-09 | 393.8 | The 2009 Legislature transferred all the money then in the fund to rebalance the 2007-09 budget | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |
  | 2009-11 | 84.3 | February 2010 special session: to keep a commitment to a $6.0 billion State School Fund | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |
  | 2009-11 | 96.4 | Rebalance of the 2009-11 budget and federal requirements to keep up spending on special education and post-secondary education | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |
  | 2011-13 | 100.0 | A 2011-12 school year account in the State School Fund that districts had to use to reduce class size or enhance learning opportunities | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |
  | 2011-13 | 82.2 | More State School Fund money and several education programs | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |
  | 2019-21 | 400.0 | Rebalance of the 2019-21 budget after the June 2020 estimate of revenue shortfalls from the COVID-19 emergency | [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3] |

  The office's 2015 edition gives the 2003 session's transfer for the final 2001-03 rebalance (the 2001-03 rebalance row
  above) as $112 million. It dates the transfers: $150 million on May 1, 2003; the 2003 rebalance in May 2003;
  the diverted lottery deposits on May 1, 2005; the $84.3 million, approved in the February 2010 special session, in May 2011; the $100 million in June 2012; and the
  $82.2 million in May 2013 [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2015-11_stabilization-funds.pdf p.3]. It also says the Legislature authorized no transfer in the 2013,
  2014 or 2015 sessions [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2015-11_stabilization-funds.pdf p.4].
- The 2020 transfer was House Bill 4303, passed in the second special session of 2020. It was offset by reductions
  from the General Fund, lottery money and the Fund for Student Success, keeping the 2019-21 State School Fund at
  $9.0 billion [SRC:fiscal/lro/2021-10_2021-measures-passed.pdf p.55]. The fund's projected balance at the end of
  2019-21 fell from $800.1 million (June 2020 forecast) to $424.4 million (September 2020 forecast), mainly because of
  the transfer [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.4].
- In 2021, House and Senate Democrats' school funding plan "hinges on the utilization of $200 million in funding from
  the Education Stability Fund" [SRC:legislature/press/2021-05-11_democrats-9.3b-school-funding-plan.pdf p.1];
  Senate Bill 226 carried the transfer [SRC:legislature/press/2021-05-11_democrats-9.3b-school-funding-plan.pdf p.2].
  Committee staff wrote that the fund "has been accessed nine times" and that the transfer would bring the State
  School Fund to $9.3 billion [SRC:legislature/olis/2021R1/241974_sb226-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1]. The State
  School Fund budget bill, Senate Bill 5514, assumed the transfer
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2021R1/61116_sb5514-budget-report.pdf p.2].
- Gov. Kate Brown objected in a May 10, 2021, letter, calling the plan "a potentially unconstitutional use of
  Education Stability Funds in the K-12 school budget"; a Legislative Counsel opinion argued that a trigger met once
  in the budget period left the fund available; Rep. Dan Rayfield, the lead budget writer, defended the plan
  [NEWS:NEWS-20210512-opb-1]. She vetoed section 5 of Senate Bill 5514,
  the $200 million limit for spending money transferred from the fund, when she signed the bill on June 15, 2021
  [SRC:legislature/laws/2021orlaw0289.pdf p.1]. Her veto letter, as quoted by The Oregonian, pointed to "the limited
  circumstances in which the Legislature can access the principal"; the fund held about $415 million in mid-May 2021.
  House Republican Leader Christine Drazan criticized the veto [NEWS:NEWS-20210617-eastoregonian-1].
- The $200 million section "did not take effect". House Bill 5006 instead raised lottery spending ($219 million) and
  Fund for Student Success spending, and reduced the General Fund appropriation ($27.8 million), keeping the State School Fund at $9.3
  billion [SRC:fiscal/lro/2021-10_2021-measures-passed.pdf p.28].
- The Legislative Fiscal Office's lottery tables show no Education Stability Fund withdrawal in 2021-23 or 2023-25
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2023R1/276164_hb5029-revenue-allocations.pdf p.3], or in 2025-27 as of the March 2026
  allocation bill, Senate Bill 5703 [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/315811_sb5703-revenue-allocations.pdf p.3].

### Proposals and positions, 2026
- Portland Public Schools asked for $500 million of the Education Stability Fund to buy down Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) rates statewide [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]. The
  district's chief financial officer said it would shrink its gap from $65 million to about $42.7 million
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261116-wweek-1].
- Rep. Ricki Ruiz, D-District 50 (Gresham) [SRC:legislature/members/ruiz.html], co-chair of the Ways and Means
  Education Subcommittee in the 2026 assignments [SRC:legislature/appointments/2026-01-23_house-committee-assignments.pdf p.3], discussed "an initial proposal to use one-time Education
  Stability Fund dollars to provide targeted PERS relief to districts experiencing significant financial strain" [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].
  No amount or bill is named [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].
- Tammy Carpenter, D-District 27 (Beaverton and Cedar Hills), who won the seat in the Nov. 3, 2026, election (unofficial results), suggested the fund as a bridge;
  Rep. Zach Hudson, D-District 49 (Troutdale, Fairview, Wood Village, Gresham, Portland), expects 2027 revenue bills
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261112-katu-2] [SRC:legislature/members/hudson.html] [SRC:legislature/members/2026-11-03_general-election-results_state-representative.html] [SRC:legislature/members/helm.html].
- Asked by OPB in October 2026 about past calls to use the fund for schools, Kotek said "That is up to the
  legislature" and, of raising it with legislative leaders, "I would bring it up, and it was summarily dismissed".
  She described their argument, which she said she could understand, that "if you use one-time dollars, which is
  what a reserve fund is, that will have a greater cliff down the line" [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1].
- At a Nov. 6, 2026, press conference Kotek said, as quoted by KATU, "To tap the one-time reserves for this particular
  instance goes against what we are talking about around enrollment-driving state investment", about Portland Public
  Schools' budget problems [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1].
- Kotek's statement on the December 2026 forecast, and statements on it from three Republican legislators
  (`decisions.md#grb-2027-29`), don't mention the Education Stability Fund or other reserves
  [SRC:gov/press/2026-11-18_kotek_revenue-forecast-statement.html]
