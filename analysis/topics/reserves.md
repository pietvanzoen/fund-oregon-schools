# Reserves: Education Stability Fund

## Analysis

Any draw from the Education Stability Fund needs a three-fifths vote in each chamber and either an economic trigger or an emergency
declaration. For a
2025-27 draw, Gov. Tina Kotek said she did not plan at that time to declare an emergency unless the votes were there. Revenue the
forecast already expects needs neither, though a tax increase also needs three-fifths of each chamber
[SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. That holds for a large, general draw. An initial proposal (November 2026) for
targeted, one-time Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) relief from Rep. Ricki Ruiz, D-District 50 (Gresham), a
co-chair of the Ways and Means Education Subcommittee in 2026, suggests a narrower
draw is being discussed, though it rests on one social media post with no amount or bill. It still needs the same
vote and trigger. The objection on record from Kotek and Sen. Kate Lieber, D-District 14 (Beaverton & Southwest
Portland) [SRC:legislature/members/lieber.html], to one-time money for ongoing costs could still apply: PERS
contributions are an ongoing cost.
The objection stated by Rep. Tawna Sanchez, D-District 43 (North and Northeast Portland)
[SRC:legislature/members/sanchez.html], is mainly timing. Ruiz describes his proposal as relief
while longer-term reform is developed (`decisions.md#esf-draw-2027-29`).

## What the sources say

- The Education Stability Fund, one of the state's two general reserve accounts, is projected at $1.271 billion at
  the end of 2025-27 [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41]. Withdrawal takes a
  three-fifths vote in each chamber plus either an economic trigger or a governor's emergency declaration
  [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1] (news coverage: [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]).
- Portland Public Schools asked for $500 million to buy down Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) rates statewide [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]. The
  district's chief financial officer said it would shrink its gap from $65 million to about $42.7 million
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261116-wweek-1].
- Rep. Ricki Ruiz, D-District 50 (Gresham) [SRC:legislature/members/ruiz.html], co-chair of the Ways and Means
  Education Subcommittee in the 2026 assignments [SRC:legislature/appointments/2026-01-23_house-committee-assignments.pdf p.3], discussed "an initial proposal to use one-time Education
  Stability Fund dollars to provide targeted PERS relief to districts experiencing significant financial strain" [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].
  No amount or bill is named [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].
- In 2021, House and Senate Democrats' school funding plan "hinges on the utilization of $200 million in funding from
  the Education Stability Fund" [SRC:legislature/press/2021-05-11_democrats-9.3b-school-funding-plan.pdf p.1]; Senate Bill 226 carried the transfer [SRC:legislature/press/2021-05-11_democrats-9.3b-school-funding-plan.pdf p.2].
- Tammy Carpenter, D-District 27 (Beaverton and Cedar Hills), who won the seat in the Nov. 3, 2026, election (unofficial results), suggested the fund as a bridge;
  Rep. Zach Hudson, D-District 49 (Troutdale, Fairview, Wood Village, Gresham, Portland), expects 2027 revenue bills
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261112-katu-2] [SRC:legislature/members/hudson.html] [SRC:legislature/members/2026-11-03_general-election-results_state-representative.html] [SRC:legislature/members/helm.html].
- Gov. Tina Kotek opposes using one-time reserves for ongoing costs [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
