# The Education Stability Fund: Oregon's school reserve

## Analysis

### What a draw needs

A draw on the Education Stability Fund's principal needs a three-fifths vote in each chamber and either one of the
economic conditions set in the Oregon Constitution (a trigger) or an emergency declared by the governor. The
constitution sets that rule for the principal only and treats the fund's investment earnings separately: they are kept
in the fund or spent on public education as state law provides
[SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. State law pays all of the fund's declared earnings out, 75% to
the Oregon Education Fund, for public education and payments on education lottery bonds, and 25% to the Oregon
Opportunity Grant, financial aid for college students; any part of the Education Fund's share that the director of the
Department of Administrative Services finds is more than needed goes to the grant as well
[SRC:legislature/ors/ors348.html]. The Legislative Fiscal Office says the bond payments were for schools approved in
the 1997 and 1999 sessions [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.2]. In November 2015 it said the debt on
those bonds had been paid off, and in 2020 it still expected any financial obligation relating to lottery bonds to end
after 2019-21; the archive doesn't show what obligation remained between those dates
[SRC:fiscal/lfo/2015-11_stabilization-funds.pdf p.2] [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3].

The Legislative Fiscal Office's lottery tables for Senate Bill 5703 (March 2026) send all of the fund's distributions
to the grant, with nothing for bond payments: $97,197,652 in the 2023-25 approved budget and $98,481,347 in the 2025-27
budget as of March 2026 [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/315811_sb5703-revenue-allocations.pdf p.3]. The December 2026
revenue forecast, released Nov. 18, 2026, projects $116.1 million in interest earnings for the 2027-29 budget, all of it
going to the grant [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.1] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.73]. The archive
has no proposal on record to redirect the earnings.

The December 2026 forecast projects the balance a draw would come from at $1,273.3 million for the end of 2025-27
(its text on p.41 still gives September's $1,271 million; below) [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.73]. In its
reserve table, which starts with 2021-23, the fund pays out about what it earns in each budget period
[SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.73]. So almost all of the balance's growth since then comes from lottery
deposits, set at 18% of net lottery proceeds until the fund reaches its cap [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.73]
[SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.72] [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. If a law kept earnings in the
fund, the archive can't say whether spending them later would need the vote and a trigger. The constitution, the
statutes, the Legislative Fiscal Office's briefs and the Legislative Revenue Office's reserve table in the archive
don't say whether kept earnings become principal, and no archived source says how earnings are declared.

For a 2025-27 draw, Gov. Tina Kotek's office said in April 2026 that she remained open to
discussing all options to support districts. It said she did not plan at that time to declare an economic emergency and call a
special session unless the presiding officers indicated three-fifths of each chamber would approve [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2] [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.3]
[NEWS:NEWS-20260416-ktvz-1]. Revenue the forecast already expects needs neither, though a tax increase
also needs three-fifths of each chamber [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. That holds for a
large, general draw.

In November 2026, Rep. Ricki Ruiz, D-District 50 (Gresham) [SRC:legislature/members/ruiz.html], a co-chair of the Ways and Means
Education Subcommittee in 2026 [SRC:legislature/appointments/2026-01-23_house-committee-assignments.pdf p.3], described an initial proposal for targeted, one-time Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) relief from
the fund. It suggests a narrower draw is being discussed, though it rests on one social media post with no amount or
bill. It still needs the same vote and trigger. Ruiz describes his proposal as relief while longer-term reform is developed
[SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].

### Whether an economic trigger is met

So far no revenue forecast for 2025-27 has met a revenue trigger, so a draw on the principal rests on the jobs
trigger or the Governor's emergency declaration, unless a 2027 forecast is low enough to meet one (below). The
constitution sets three triggers: a quarterly forecast putting current-budget General Fund revenue at least 2% below
the forecast the budget was built on (the 2% test); a decline in payroll jobs for two or more quarters in a row within
the last 12 months (the jobs test); or the budget period's last forecast putting General Fund money for the next budget
at least 3% below current appropriations (the 3% test) [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html].

The jobs test is the open one. The state's forecasters, the Office of Economic Analysis, first estimated that Oregon
payroll jobs fell in January-March and again in April-June 2026, which would be two quarters in a row. But the
September 2026 forecast raised the January-March figure, and the archive has no figure from that forecast for the
quarter before it, so it can't say whether January-March still shows a decline (below). The forecasters wrote only
that revisions back to April 2025 changed little in the direction or magnitude of Oregon's job market overall
[SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-09_forecast.pdf p.13].

The 2% test hasn't been met by any of the six 2025-27 forecasts through the December 2026 revenue forecast, released
Nov. 18, 2026. The largest decline in gross General Fund revenue (all General Fund tax and other revenue, before
transfers out) from the close-of-session forecast (the forecast the 2025-27 budget was built on, $35,759.8 million)
was 1.76%, in the September 2025 forecast, released Aug. 27, 2025 [SRC:fiscal/oea/2025-09_forecast.pdf p.27]
[DER:reserves/esf_2pct_gross_max_decline]. The forecasters put that drop down mostly to the federal tax law H.R. 1,
with economic revisions and a changed kicker credit also playing a part [SRC:fiscal/oea/2025-09_forecast.pdf p.6]
[SRC:fiscal/oea/2025-09_forecast.pdf p.27]. Every forecast since June 2026 has put revenue above the close-of-session
forecast. Available resources, which add the balance carried in from 2023-25, fell 2.24% in September 2025
[DER:reserves/esf_2pct_resources_2025-09], but the constitution's test is about revenue.

The 3% test uses the last forecast of the budget period, which the forecasters' tentative calendar puts on May 12,
2027 [SRC:fiscal/oea/2027_forecast-calendar.pdf p.1] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.33]. In the December
2026 forecast, 2027-29 net General Fund revenue (gross revenue less transfers out), $42,592.8 million, is about 13.5%
more than the 2025-27 General Fund appropriations of $37,513.1 million, our estimate, which leaves out the beginning
balance [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.33] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.29]
[DER:reserves/esf_3pct_2026-12]. Meeting the test would take the May 2027 forecast of 2027-29 revenue to fall about
14.6% below the December 2026 figure, our estimate, assuming appropriations stay as they are
[DER:reserves/esf_3pct_drop_needed_2026-12]. For 2027-29 itself, the 2% test can't be applied until the 2027-29
close-of-session forecast exists, after the 2027 session.

If the Legislature finds the jobs test met, a draw on the principal needs the three-fifths votes but no emergency
declaration. The Governor can still veto single items in an appropriation bill
[SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html], as Gov. Kate Brown did with spending from the fund in 2021
(below). If the Legislature
doesn't find a trigger met, a draw also needs an emergency declaration. In April 2026, asked about a draw in the
middle of the 2025-27 budget, Kotek's office said she did not then plan to declare one and call a special session
unless the presiding officers indicated three-fifths support in each chamber (see "What a draw needs"). The archive has
no statement from her on a declaration during the 2027 session.

The Legislature makes the finding, and no archived source from the Legislative Fiscal Office, the Legislative
Revenue Office, the forecasters or Legislative Counsel (the Legislature's lawyers) says whether any trigger is met
now. The Oregon School Boards Association and the Coalition of Oregon School Administrators, in an April 2026 report,
said the 2026 Legislature used neither the Education Stability Fund nor the Rainy Day Fund to balance the budget
"despite meeting the economic triggers to access those funds"; they didn't say which triggers
[SRC:orgs/osba-cosa/2026-04-08_legislative-report.pdf p.4]. Their report came out before the June 2026 forecast,
released May 20, 2026, the first to show the January-March 2026 decline [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-06_forecast.pdf p.1].
Before then the archive has two figures that come closest: the 2.24% fall in available resources from the close-of-session
forecast in September 2025, a measure the 2% test doesn't use (above), and a 1.5% drop, at an annual rate, in payroll
jobs in April-June 2025. The archive lacks the quarter before it, January-March 2025; if that quarter also fell, the
two would make two quarters in a row, though it ended more than 12 months before their report
[SRC:fiscal/oea/2025-09_forecast.pdf p.44] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2025-09_forecast.pdf p.46]. The archive can't tell what
they relied on.

Timing is the other open point. The jobs test counts declines "in the last 12 months"
[SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html], and January-March 2026 ended March 31, 2026; the archive
has no source on how those 12 months are counted for a finding in the 2027 session. In 2021 a Legislative Counsel
opinion argued that a trigger met earlier in the budget period left the fund available, and Gov. Kate Brown vetoed
the draw anyway (below). Nor does the archive say whether a trigger found in 2025-27 would support spending in the
2027-29 budget.

### Past draws and the 2021 veto

No money has been taken from the fund since the 2020 transfer, and none of its past uses paid for pension costs
(below). The Legislative Fiscal Office lists nine allocations, which total $1,540.6 million, not adjusted for
inflation [DER:reserves/esf_withdrawals_total]. They went to the State School Fund or other education programs,
mostly to balance or rebalance the state budget in the downturns of 2001-03, 2007-11 and 2020.

The last draw the Legislature wrote into a budget bill, $200 million for the 2021-23 State School Fund, did not take
effect. Gov. Kate Brown vetoed it. She argued that the conditions for using the fund's principal, a downturn or
another emergency affecting school budgets, were not met. A Legislative Counsel opinion had argued that a trigger
met earlier in the budget period left the fund available. Lawmakers kept the State School Fund at $9.3 billion
mainly with lottery money instead.

The Legislative Fiscal Office's lottery tables show no withdrawal in 2021-23, 2023-25 or 2025-27, as of March 2026.
If the Legislature draws on the fund in 2027-29, then it would be the first withdrawal since 2020. A draw for pension
costs would be the first in the record. The 2021 veto shows that a governor's reading of the conditions can stop
spending the Legislature has budgeted from the fund.

### What a draw for budget gaps would cost

What a one-time draw for districts' budget gaps would cost statewide isn't known. The archive has no statewide total
of district budget gaps for 2027-28; the only district figure is Portland Public Schools' own projection of a $65
million gap. The district's $500 million request for all districts' pension rates would be about 39% of the $1,273.3
million balance projected for the end of 2025-27 in the December 2026 forecast [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.73]
[DER:reserves/pps_ask_share_of_esf].

## What the sources say

- The Education Stability Fund, one of the state's two general reserve accounts, is projected at $1,273.3 million at
  the end of 2025-27 in the December 2026 forecast's reserve table [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.73]. The
  forecast's text gives $1,271 million for the fund, $2,192 million for the Rainy Day Fund and $3,463 million in total
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.41]. The September 2026 forecast's reserve table gives $1,271.4 million,
  $2,192.0 million and $3,463.3 million [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-09_forecast.pdf p.68]; the December table gives
  $1,273.3 million, $2,203.1 million and $3,476.4 million [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.73]. Withdrawal takes a
  three-fifths vote in each chamber plus either an economic trigger or a governor's emergency declaration
  [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1] (news coverage: [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]).
- The Oregon Constitution sets that vote and trigger for "any portion of the principal" of the fund. Of its earnings
  it says they "shall be retained in the fund or expended for the public purpose of financing public education in
  Oregon as provided by law" [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. The Legislative Fiscal Office
  describes the vote and trigger as the conditions to access the principal [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.2],
  and says the retention of earnings in the fund and their use for public education are constitutional requirements,
  while the actual distribution is set in statute [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3].
- Withdrawals must be used for public education; the fund has no repayment provision; its cap is 5% of General Fund
  revenue in the prior budget period [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1]. The Legislative Fiscal Office
  says public education here means K-12 and post-secondary education, and that some public early learning programs may
  be eligible [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.2].
  Unlike the Rainy Day Fund, the Education Stability Fund has no limit of two-thirds of its balance per budget period
  [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1].
- The fund held $1,012 million on July 1, 2025, and $1,041 million on Oct. 1, 2025
  [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1].

### The fund's earnings

- Under Oregon Revised Statutes (ORS) 348.696, all "declared earnings" on the fund, including those on the Oregon Growth Account within it, are
  transferred and continuously appropriated: 75% to the Oregon Education Fund and 25% to the Higher Education
  Coordinating Commission for the Oregon Opportunity Grant program. If the director of the Department of
  Administrative Services determines that the Oregon Education Fund's share in a budget period exceeds what is needed
  for public education and education lottery bond debt service, the excess goes to the Opportunity Grant as well.
  Under ORS 348.716, the Oregon Education Fund is continuously appropriated to the department for public education and
  education lottery bond debt service [SRC:legislature/ors/ors348.html].
- The Legislative Fiscal Office wrote in 2020 that the earnings had paid debt service on bonds for schools approved in
  the 1997 and 1999 sessions, and that the Opportunity Grant provides financial assistance to post-secondary students
  [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.2]. It said the Legislature amended ORS 348.696 in 2015 so that
  Oregon Education Fund transfers not needed for debt service go to the Opportunity Grant, and that lottery bond
  obligations were expected to end after 2019-21, with no further transfer to the Oregon Education Fund required
  [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.3]. The office's November 2015 brief on the state's
  stabilization funds says the debt on the 1997 and 1999 school bonds had been paid off, and that when there is no
  education lottery bond debt service, or it needs less than 75% of the earnings, the earnings set aside for it become
  available to the commission for opportunity grants [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2015-11_stabilization-funds.pdf p.2].
- The Legislative Fiscal Office's lottery tables for Senate Bill 5703 (March 2026) list the fund's distributions as
  going to the Oregon Opportunity Grant Program: $97,197,652 in the 2023-25 approved budget and $98,481,347 in the
  2025-27 budget as of March 2026, with no debt service allocation to the Department of Education and no withdrawal
  in either period. The table adds the Oregon Growth Account's earnings distributions to the fund's revenue, though
  it leaves out the account's balances [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/315811_sb5703-revenue-allocations.pdf p.3]. The
  forecast's table below leaves that subaccount out and was released in November 2026; its figures differ from these
  [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.1] [SRC:fiscal/oea/2026-12_forecast.pdf p.73].

### The economic triggers in 2025-27

- The constitution lets the Legislature spend the fund's principal on public education with a three-fifths vote in
  each house if it finds one of three conditions: the last quarterly forecast for a budget period shows money
  available for the next one at least 3% less than the current period's General Fund appropriations; "a decline for two or more
  consecutive quarters in the last 12 months in seasonally adjusted nonfarm payroll employment"; or a quarterly
  forecast projects current-period General Fund revenue at least 2% below the forecast the adopted budget was based on. The other route is a three-fifths
  vote and the Governor's emergency declaration [SRC:legislature/constitution/oregon-constitution.html]. The
  Legislative Revenue Office's February 2026 summary describes the same triggers, and says the 3% test compares
  General Fund revenue in the next period with current appropriations [SRC:fiscal/lro/2026-02_basic-facts.pdf p.18].

  The office's 2015 edition gives the 2003 session's transfer for the final 2001-03 rebalance (the 2001-03 rebalance row
  above) as $112 million. It gives dates for the transfers. The $150 million was set for May 1, 2003, and
  the 2003 rebalance moved in May 2003. The diverted lottery deposits moved on May 1, 2005. The $84.3 million,
  approved in the February 2010 special session, moved in May 2011. The $100 million was set for October 2011 and then
  changed to June 2012, and the $82.2 million was set for May 2013 [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2015-11_stabilization-funds.pdf p.3]. It also says the Legislature authorized no transfer in the 2013,
  2014 or 2015 sessions [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2015-11_stabilization-funds.pdf p.4].
- The 2020 transfer was House Bill 4303, passed in the second special session of 2020. It was offset by reductions
  from the General Fund, lottery money and the Fund for Student Success, keeping the 2019-21 State School Fund at
  $9.0 billion [SRC:fiscal/lro/2021-10_2021-measures-passed.pdf p.55]. The fund's projected balance at the end of
  2019-21 fell from $800.1 million (June 2020 forecast) to $424.4 million (September 2020 forecast), mainly because of
  the transfer [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2020-09_budget-reserve-funds.pdf p.4].
- In 2021, House and Senate Democrats' school funding plan "hinges on the utilization of $200 million in funding from
  the Education Stability Fund" [SRC:legislature/press/2021-05-11_democrats-9.3b-school-funding-plan.pdf p.1];
  Senate Bill 226 carried the transfer [SRC:legislature/press/2021-05-11_democrats-9.3b-school-funding-plan.pdf p.2].
  Committee staff wrote that the fund "has been accessed nine times" and that the transfer would bring the State
  School Fund to $9.3 billion [SRC:legislature/olis/2021R1/241974_sb226-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1]. The State
  School Fund budget bill, Senate Bill 5514, assumed the transfer
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2021R1/61116_sb5514-budget-report.pdf p.2].
- Gov. Kate Brown objected in a May 10, 2021, letter, calling the plan "a potentially unconstitutional use of
  Education Stability Funds in the K-12 school budget"; a Legislative Counsel opinion argued that a trigger met once
  in the budget period left the fund available; Rep. Dan Rayfield, the lead budget writer, defended the plan
  [NEWS:NEWS-20210512-opb-1]. She vetoed section 5 of Senate Bill 5514,
  the $200 million limit for spending money transferred from the fund, when she signed the bill on June 15, 2021
  [SRC:legislature/laws/2021orlaw0289.pdf p.1]. Her veto letter, as quoted by The Oregonian, pointed to "the limited
  circumstances in which the Legislature can access the principal"; the fund held about $415 million in mid-May 2021.
  House Republican Leader Christine Drazan criticized the veto [NEWS:NEWS-20210617-eastoregonian-1].
- The $200 million section "did not take effect". House Bill 5006 instead raised lottery spending ($219 million) and
  Fund for Student Success spending, and reduced the General Fund appropriation ($27.8 million), keeping the State School Fund at $9.3
  billion [SRC:fiscal/lro/2021-10_2021-measures-passed.pdf p.28].
- The Legislative Fiscal Office's lottery tables show no Education Stability Fund withdrawal in 2021-23 or 2023-25
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2023R1/276164_hb5029-revenue-allocations.pdf p.3], or in 2025-27 as of the March 2026
  allocation bill, Senate Bill 5703 [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/315811_sb5703-revenue-allocations.pdf p.3].

### Proposals and positions, 2026
- In an FAQ released with Executive Order 26-06 in April 2026 [NEWS:NEWS-20260416-ktvz-1], the Governor's office
  said that "The Governor remains open to discussing all options to support districts" but that "at this time she does not plan to declare an economic emergency and call a special session" to access
  the fund unless the presiding officers indicate that three-fifths of members serving in each house would approve
  [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2] [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.3].
- At a May 7, 2026, school funding forum, as reported by Willamette Week, Ways and Means co-chairs Sen. Kate Lieber
  and Rep. Tawna Sanchez said they saw no reason to tap the fund that year. Lieber said "If you use one-time money to
  pay for ongoing expenses, then you're still going to have a cliff"; Sanchez said worse years are coming as federal
  changes shift social-service costs to the state in 2027-29 and 2029-31 [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1].
- Portland Public Schools asked for $500 million of the Education Stability Fund to buy down Public Employees Retirement System (PERS) rates statewide [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]. The
  district's chief financial officer said it would shrink its gap from $65 million to about $42.7 million
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261116-wweek-1].
- Rep. Ricki Ruiz, D-District 50 (Gresham) [SRC:legislature/members/ruiz.html], co-chair of the Ways and Means
  Education Subcommittee in the 2026 assignments [SRC:legislature/appointments/2026-01-23_house-committee-assignments.pdf p.3], discussed "an initial proposal to use one-time Education
  Stability Fund dollars to provide targeted PERS relief to districts experiencing significant financial strain" [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].
  No amount or bill is named [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].
- Tammy Carpenter, D-District 27 (Beaverton and Cedar Hills), who won the seat in the Nov. 3, 2026, election (unofficial results), suggested the fund as a bridge;
  Rep. Zach Hudson, D-District 49 (Troutdale, Fairview, Wood Village, Gresham, Portland), expects 2027 revenue bills
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261112-katu-2] [SRC:legislature/members/hudson.html] [SRC:legislature/members/2026-11-03_general-election-results_state-representative.html] [SRC:legislature/members/helm.html].
- Asked by OPB in October 2026 about past calls to use the fund for schools, Kotek said "That is up to the
  legislature" and, of raising it with legislative leaders, "I would bring it up, and it was summarily dismissed".

  Schools' budget problems [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1].
