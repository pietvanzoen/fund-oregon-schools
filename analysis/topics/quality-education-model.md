# The Quality Education Model: the state's estimate of what schools need

Decisions this informs: `decisions.md#ssf-2027-29`, `decisions.md#cost-model-2027`. Figures copied from sources:
`data/qem_gap.csv`, `data/ssf_history.csv`.

## What the sources say

### The 2027-29 figures

- The 2026 Quality Education Model (QEM) report puts the State School Fund (SSF) current service level (CSL) at
  $12,558,429,348, the Department of Administrative Services' estimate including the SSA transfer
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103].
- It puts the core state funding needed to keep 2025-27 service levels (the "status quo") at $13.78B, and the core
  state funding needed to meet the state's goals (the "full QEM") at $16.21B [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
- The full QEM is $2.42B more than the status quo and $3.65B more than "the SSF CSL estimate available at the time
  of this report's publication" [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
- The status quo counts state funding beyond the SSF: $1,217,461,216 of it is non-SSF state money
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103].
- The Quality Education Commission leaves "the determination of adequacy and which state funding streams and grant
  programs should count toward that to the Legislature" [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].
- The Legislature adopted a $11.36B SSF for 2025-27 [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2025-07_lab-summary-2025-27.pdf p.1], described
  in a committee press release as $11.4B [SRC:legislature/press/2025-05-28_record-k12-funding.pdf p.1]. Kotek had
  proposed $11.36B [NEWS:NEWS-20241203-osba-1].
- The PPS Board cites a $12.57B CSL [PPSARCH:BB:14673756 p.1].
- News coverage reported the QEM need as "$2.5B more" and "$5B more" [NEWS:NEWS-20260812-opb-1].
- By 2024 Census data, Oregon ranked 20th in state-sourced per-pupil funding; funding at the QEM's level would move it
  to 12th [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].

### Whether the QEM is the right yardstick

- The Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation's Nov 2025 report says 2025-27 funding "effectively closes the
  funding gap and allows for the full implementation of the QEM" [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.4].
- The same report criticizes how the QEM is built [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.5].
- A JPEA legislative concept would move the cost model from the Quality Education Commission to the Legislative
  Policy and Research Office [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/311607_jpea-legislative-concepts.pdf p.1]. It became
  SB 1555 (2026), described by Rep. Ruiz as replacing the QEM [NEWS:NEWS-20260121-katu-1].
- The overhaul effort led by Ruiz and Sen. Sollman was unsuccessful, and Sen. Lew Frederick said he expects
  significant changes in 2027 [NEWS:NEWS-20260617-occ-1].
- In Nov 2026, Ruiz described a long-term vision of a new funding system with an adequate resource needs cost model
  that increases the number of prototype school models [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].

## Analysis

The three 2027-29 figures answer different questions. The CSL is the State School Fund alone; the status quo and the
full QEM count other state money too. So "$3.65B above the CSL" compares a fund with a broader total, and
"$2.42B above the status quo" is the like-for-like gap between the two QEM scenarios.

Record funding in 2025-27 and a gap in 2027-29 can both be true: they measure different two-year budgets against
different estimates. News figures of "$2.5B more" and "$5B more" appear to compare the QEM with the 2025-27 budget,
not with the CSL, so they aren't comparable with the figures above.

The QEM is the state's own estimate, but it is contested: the Legislature's committee found 2025-27 funding sufficient
by it while criticizing its method, and the 2027 Legislature may replace it. A gap measured against the QEM should be
read as a gap against one benchmark.

Per-student spending: a claim that Oregon spends above the national average per student is not yet sourced here. The
QEM report's ranking is of state-sourced funding, which is a different measure from total spending.
