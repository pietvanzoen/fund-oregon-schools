# The funding formula: how the State School Fund is shared

Decisions this informs: `decisions.md#formula-2027`. Open research: #17, #18, #19, #25.

## Analysis

As of Nov. 22, 2026, the archive has no formula proposal from Gov. Tina Kotek. Her Nov. 6 remarks float "student need" as a
possible basis, with no design, cost or timeline, and she framed it as a longer conversation.

If a formula change is proposed in 2027, it would go to the House Education Committee and the Senate Committee on
Education, and its money to the Ways and Means Education Subcommittee, so their 2027 chairs matter. On Nov. 22, 2026,
a web search of oregonlegislature.gov found no 2027 committee assignment memos, only 2026 ones; #107 picks them up
once they are posted. Sen. Janeen Sollman, D-District 15 (Forest Grove, Cornelius, Hillsboro, Rock Creek), the
2026 Senate co-chair of the Education Subcommittee, will not return (she lost her
May 2026 primary [NEWS:NEWS-20260521-wweek-1]), so that seat will change. Neither chamber's 2027 subcommittee
co-chairs are in the archive's sources yet.

Still to research: the final status of House Bill 2953 (2025), which as introduced would remove the special
education cap (#17), how closures change transportation costs (#18), the English learner
and poverty weights (#19), and the 2027 reform proposals (#25).

## What the sources say

- The formula in chapter 327 of the Oregon Revised Statutes divides the State School Fund among districts [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html].
- **Special education cap.** The formula adds weight for students eligible for special education only up to 11% of
  a district's enrollment without review and approval by the Department of Education [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html].
- House Bill 2953 (2025) as introduced would remove the cap [SRC:legislature/bills/2025R1_HB2953_introduced.pdf p.1].
- **Transportation.** The state reimburses an approved share of district transportation costs
  [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html].
- **Charters.** A sponsoring district must pass through at least 80% (K-8) or 95% (9-12) of its General Purpose
  Grant per weighted student to a public charter school it sponsors, virtual ones included [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html]; see
  `topics/virtual-charters.md`.
- At a Nov. 6, 2026, press conference Gov. Tina Kotek said, as quoted by KATU: "Maybe enrollment is the wrong
  marker. That was established almost 30 years ago. Maybe it has to be student need." She called rethinking it "a
  longer conversation than what is happening in districts right now" [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1].
- **Enrollment as the basis.** The formula counts students by weighted average daily membership: the district's
  average enrollment plus extra weight for some students, such as 1.0 for each student in special education and 0.5
  for each English learner [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html].
