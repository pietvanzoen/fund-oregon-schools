# Online charters: State School Fund money that follows students

Issue #20. Data: `data/virtual_charters_2025-26.csv`.

## What the law says (ORS chapter 338)

- A "virtual public charter school" is a public charter school that provides online courses; it does not include a
  charter school that primarily serves students in a physical location [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html]
  (ORS 338.005(5)).
- **Students count where the charter is, not where they live.** For State School Fund purposes, charter students
  are treated as residents of the district in which the charter is located, and the SSF money for them is paid
  first to that district [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html] (ORS 338.155(1)(a)-(b)). The law sets no separate rate
  for virtual charters: online and brick-and-mortar charters have the same minimum payment rates (the separate ADMw
  calculation below applies only to virtual charters).
- **Sponsor's minimum pass-through.** A sponsoring district must contract to pay its charter at least 80% of the
  district's General Purpose Grant per ADMw for students in K-8 and at least 95% for grades 9-12; the two sides may
  agree on more [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html] (ORS 338.155(2), (7)). The district keeps whatever it does not
  pass through. (Only for charters sponsored by the State Board must the district send half of the unpaid portion
  to ODE [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html] (ORS 338.155(3), (5)).)
- **Poverty weight is borrowed from the sponsor.** A charter's ADMw assumes the same share of children in poverty
  as the district where it is located [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html] (ORS 338.157).
- **Separate calculation for shrinking sponsors.** If a sponsoring district's own enrollment (leaving out the
  virtual charter) fell from the year before, ODE calculates the virtual charter's ADMw separately from the
  district's when working out the district's extended ADMw [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html] (ORS
  338.155(1)(c)(C)). ODE publishes this calculation in a "Virtual Charter School Ext ADMw Transparency Report"
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.1].
- **The 3% rule.** A student does not need the resident district's approval to enroll in a virtual charter, only to
  give notice of intent and of enrollment. But if more than 3% of a district's resident students already attend
  virtual charters the district does not sponsor, the district may refuse approval; it must then tell the family
  the current percentage, the right to appeal to the State Board, and other online options
  [SRC:legislature/ors/ors338.html] (ORS 338.125(4)-(5)). ODE's guidance dates the option to deny to HB 2301 (2011),
  with timelines adjusted by HB 3204 (2023) [SRC:ode/charter/2026-08_virtual-charter-enrollment-guidance.pdf p.3].
- **No Student Investment Account money.** Virtual charters cannot apply for SIA grants under the Student Success
  Act, and ODE removes their ADMw from their sponsors' SIA allocations; ODE's September 2026 list for 2027-29
  names 24 virtual charters and their sponsors [SRC:ode/charter/2027-29_sia-virtual-charter-list.pdf p.1].

## How many students and how much money (2025-26)

- ODE's 2025-26 transparency report (second-period ADM, dated Apr 27, 2026) lists 20 virtual charters sponsored
  by 17 districts [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.2]. Summed from that report:
  - ADMr (resident enrollment) of 22,110 in 2025-26, up from 20,413 in 2024-25;
  - ADMw (weighted) of 22,714 in 2025-26, up from 21,035.
  ODE's later 2027-29 SIA list names four virtual charters that are not in this report (Applegate Valley, Jefferson
  Classical, Lower Columbia, Stellar Pines) [SRC:ode/charter/2027-29_sia-virtual-charter-list.pdf p.1]; if any of them
  operated in 2025-26, the totals here undercount.
  The largest are Baker Web Academy (3,840 ADMr), Fossil Charter School (3,295) and Oregon Charter Academy
  (2,601) [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.2].
- ODE's May 2026 estimate puts statewide ADMr at 531,425 and ADMw at 668,444 for 2025-26
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_ssf-estimates_2026-05-01.pdf p.1]. Analysis: virtual charters are about 4.2% of ADMr and
  3.4% of ADMw. Their weighted count is close to their raw count. That says little about the students themselves:
  a charter's poverty weight is copied from its sponsor (ORS 338.157, above), and the statewide ADMw includes
  hold-harmless "extended" weight that isn't comparable.
- Separately, the 2026 QEM report counts 52 virtual schools with 27,978 students (5.2%) in 2024-25
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.16]. Analysis: the report doesn't say how it defines a virtual school or how it
  counts students; with 52 schools against 20 virtual charters it likely includes non-charter online schools, so it
  should not be compared one-for-one with charter ADMr. OJP's figures (about 31,000 students in "online programs",
  76 virtual schools) are as of the article (Nov 2026) and use its own definitions [NEWS:NEWS-20261103-wweek-1].
  The three sets of numbers measure different things and are not in conflict.
- The report marks "Yes" for a separate calculation in 13 sponsoring districts and "No" in four (Prairie City,
  Paisley, Fossil, Mitchell) [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.2].
- In several sponsors the virtual charter is most of the district. In Fossil SD, the charter's 3,347 ADMw against
  193 for the district itself; in Prairie City, 1,410 ADMw across two virtual charters against 542; in Baker SD,
  3,915 against 2,242 [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.1]
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.2].
- **No ODE dollar total.** None of the archived ODE documents gives the SSF dollars attributable to virtual
  charters. What they do give is each sponsor's General Purpose Grant per extended ADMw: $10,801 (Mitchell) to
  $11,397 (Paisley) among the 17 sponsors in 2025-26 [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_ssf-estimates_2026-05-01.pdf p.191]
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_ssf-estimates_2026-05-01.pdf p.93]. Each district page also shows a "Charter Schools Rate
  (ORS 338.155)", which ODE does not define on the page and which equals or exceeds the General Purpose Grant
  rate (e.g. $11,514 vs $11,129 for North Bend) [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_ssf-estimates_2026-05-01.pdf p.32].
  Analysis: for North Bend it equals the General Purpose Grant ($36,777,648) divided by current-year ADMw (3,194.12)
  rather than extended ADMw (3,304.67) [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_ssf-estimates_2026-05-01.pdf p.32], which would explain
  why it is never below the grant rate; not confirmed by ODE.
- Analysis (estimate, not an ODE figure): multiplying each virtual charter's extended ADMw in the transparency
  report by its sponsor's General Purpose Grant per extended ADMw gives about **$257M** of formula revenue for
  2025-26, roughly 3.3% of the $7.88B school district share of 2025-26 formula revenue (total formula revenue,
  ESDs included, is $8.25B) [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_ssf-estimates_2026-05-01.pdf p.1]. Those are one year of state
  plus local formula revenue, not the State School Fund appropriation ($5.57B for
  2025-26 on the same page, the first year of the $11.36B 2025-27 appropriation). The estimate likewise mixes state and local money (the grant is calculated before
  local revenue is subtracted), leaves out the transportation grant and high-cost disability grants, and uses each
  sponsor's average rate. Row-by-row figures are
  in `data/virtual_charters_2025-26.csv`.
- **The sponsors' share.** How much a sponsor keeps depends on each charter contract, which isn't archived, and
  the transparency report does not split students between K-8 and 9-12. Analysis: if every contract paid exactly
  the statutory minimum, sponsors would keep between 5% (all high school) and 20% (all K-8) of the grant, i.e.
  roughly $13M-$51M of the $257M estimate. Contracts may pay more than the minimum, which would lower that; and the
  pass-through applies to current-year ADMw while the estimate uses extended ADMw, which could raise it a little.

## What the news reported

- The Oregon Journalism Project's investigation (published Nov 3, 2026 in Willamette Week) says about 31,000
  students, roughly 6% of K-12, are in the state's online programs, and that state money follows each student
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261103-wweek-1]. Its main example, Nyssa's online program, is set up by the district with a
  for-profit company and does not appear in ODE's virtual charter report
  [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_virtual-charter-ext-admw-transparency_2026-04-27.pdf p.1]. Analysis: if it is not a
  charter, the ORS 338.155 pass-through minimums would not govern that arrangement. Under its contract Nyssa kept 40% of the
  per-student money and the company got 60% [NEWS:NEWS-20261103-wweek-1].
- The article says each student who leaves a brick-and-mortar school for a virtual one costs that school more than
  $11,000 [NEWS:NEWS-20261103-wweek-1]. Analysis: that is in line with the 2025-26 General Purpose Grant per ADMw
  ($10,801-$11,397 among virtual charter sponsors, above). It is the revenue that moves with the student, not a net
  cost to the system.
- The article also reports families who say online school works for them [NEWS:NEWS-20261103-wweek-1].
- Kotek's statement to OJP said she "is actively reviewing the state's current approach to virtual education"
  [NEWS:NEWS-20261103-wweek-1]. As of Nov 20, 2026, no proposal from her on virtual schools is in the archive; her
  2027-29 budget is due Dec 1.

## Analysis: what this means for other districts' SSF share

- What ODE distributes by ADMw is total formula revenue: the fixed state appropriation plus local revenue, $8.25B in
  2025-26 [SRC:ode/ssf/2025-26_ssf-estimates_2026-05-01.pdf p.1].
  A student who moves from a district school to a virtual charter mostly moves their weight from one district's
  count to the sponsor's rather than adding to the statewide total.
- Where other districts' shares can change: (a) students who were not in public school before (homeschooled or
  private) add new ADMw and spread the fixed state pool more thinly; (b) the extended-ADMw hold-harmless can count
  the student in the resident district's prior-year figure while the sponsor counts them this year.
  (c) The charter borrows the sponsor's poverty share
  (ORS 338.157), so a sponsor with a higher poverty share than the students' home districts adds weight statewide,
  and a lower one subtracts it; (d) the same hold-harmless on the sponsor's side, which the separate calculation
  in ORS 338.155(1)(c)(C) limits. None of these can be sized from the archived documents.
- The larger questions are what the money buys: the share sponsors keep (above), what the charters' operators
  spend it on, and outcomes. Those are outside this issue; see open questions.
