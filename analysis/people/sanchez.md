# Rep. Tawna Sanchez

Official page: <https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/sanchez>

Rep. Tawna Sanchez is a Democrat representing House District 43 (North and Northeast Portland)
[SRC:legislature/members/sanchez.html]. She is House co-chair of the Joint Committee on Ways and Means
[SRC:legislature/appointments/2026-01-23_house-committee-assignments.pdf p.3].
Her 2027 role isn't confirmed yet: House Democrats haven't announced 2027 committee leadership (as of Nov. 19, 2026).
Part of the Portland delegation.

## Role in K-12 budget decisions
- Co-chaired the 2025-27 budget with Sen. Kate Lieber, D-District 14 (Beaverton & Southwest Portland)
  [SRC:legislature/members/lieber.html], including the co-chairs' framework for $11.4 billion for the State School Fund
  [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2025-03_co-chair-budget-framework.pdf p.2] [SRC:legislature/press/2025-03-19_jwm-framework.pdf p.1].
- On the full Ways and Means Committee she voted yes on Senate Bill 141's budget [SRC:legislature/bills/2025R1_SB141_budget-report.pdf p.1].
- Co-led the 2026 rebalance [SRC:legislature/press/2026-03-01_jwm-rebalance.pdf p.1].

## On revenue and the budget
- On the 2026 rebalance: "Our priority continues to be maintaining the essential services that Oregonians rely on,
  like education, health care, and public safety" [SRC:legislature/press/2026-03-01_jwm-rebalance.pdf p.2].
- Same release: "While this budget rebalance was relatively benign, we know there will be difficult decisions in the
  years ahead due to the disastrous policies of the federal administration." [SRC:legislature/press/2026-03-01_jwm-rebalance.pdf p.2]

## On the Education Stability Fund
- At a May 7, 2026, forum, she argued against tapping it yet: "It's actually not raining quite yet. The hurricane has not hit
  yet." [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1]
- Same forum: said federal H.R. 1 cuts could shift hundreds of millions, possibly over $1 billion, in social-service costs
  onto the state in 2027-29 and 2029-31, and pointed to COVID relief money spent on routine costs as a warning
  against one-time fixes [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1].
- She also stressed her interest in revenue reform and warned that relying on one-time dollars is risky (reporter's
  paraphrase) [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1].
- Analysis: her objection is about timing (she said worse years are coming, as federal H.R. 1 could shift
  social-service costs to the state in 2027-29 and 2029-31), alongside a caution about relying on one-time money
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1].
