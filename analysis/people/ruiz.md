# Rep. Ricki Ruiz

Official page: <https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/ruiz>

Democrat, House District 50 (Gresham). House co-chair of the Ways and Means Subcommittee on Education, which
writes the State School Fund and Department of Education budgets
[SRC:legislature/appointments/2026-01-23_house-committee-assignments.pdf p.3], and co-chair of the Joint Committee on
Public Education Appropriation (JPEA), which reports on whether school funding is sufficient
[SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.2] [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.3]. (The JPEA report
spells his name "Ricky".) His 2027 committee roles aren't announced yet (as of Nov 19, 2026).

## Role in K-12 budget decisions
- Presiding co-chair at the subcommittee's March 27, 2025 public hearing on SB 5516, the 2025-27 State School Fund
  bill [SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/recordinglog_jwmed_2025-03-27.pdf p.1].
- On the full Ways and Means Committee he voted yes on SB 141's budget [SRC:legislature/bills/2025R1_SB141_budget-report.pdf p.1].
- Co-chaired the JPEA when it adopted its Nov 2025 report, which states that 2025-27 state funding "effectively
  closes the funding gap and allows for the full implementation of the QEM" [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.4].
  The same report criticizes the QEM's method: goals lacking specificity, a costing approach that doesn't meet best
  practice, revenue sources left out of the gap calculation [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310695_jpea.pdf p.5].
- With Sen. Sollman, led SB 1555 (2026), which would have replaced the Quality Education Commission and model with a
  cost model developed by the Legislative Policy and Research Office via contract and tied quality goals to SB 141 metrics
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/311607_jpea-legislative-concepts.pdf p.1] [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/313691_sb1555-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1]. Senate Education took it up on Feb 10 and Feb 12, 2026
  [SRC:legislature/olis/2026R1/313691_sb1555-staff-measure-summary.pdf p.1].
  The overhaul effort was unsuccessful [NEWS:NEWS-20260617-occ-1]: OLIS lists SB 1555 as still in the Senate
  Committee on Education, with no chapter number [SRC:legislature/bills/2026R1_SB1555_overview.html], and the
  commission's Oct 2026 materials list it as "Did not pass" [SRC:ode/qec/minutes/2026-10-21.pdf p.78]. See
  `topics/quality-education-model.md` for what the bill would have done.

## On school funding
- On the 2025-27 SSF, in a joint release from the Senate President and House Speaker: "The end of Covid-era relief
  funds and declining student populations are putting our schools in a challenging situation"
  [SRC:legislature/press/2025-05-28_record-k12-funding.pdf p.2].
- On the funding model: "Every year we have this debate of what is adequate funding for public schools in the state
  of Oregon" [NEWS:NEWS-20260121-katu-1].
- Same interview: "We're not repeating the same cycle and expecting different results" [NEWS:NEWS-20260121-katu-1].

## Nov 2026 proposals
- Nov 19, 2026, in his own Instagram post after an Oregon School Boards Association (OSBA) panel: "Our long-term vision is to build a new education funding system that is equitable, targeted,
  and tailored to the unique needs of each region of Oregon" [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt]. It includes "an adequate resource needs
  cost model that increases the number of prototype school models" (the QEM costs schooling from prototype schools
  [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.93]) [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt]. He says what he heard at
  James John Elementary reinforced his belief in the immediate relief approach [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt].

## On the Education Stability Fund
- Same post, among the work he says he "previewed": "We also discussed" "an initial proposal to use one-time Education Stability Fund dollars to provide targeted PERS relief to
  districts experiencing significant financial strain, particularly those that have already tapped into their own
  reserves" [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt]. On why now: "Our districts cannot wait years for a better funding system while
  making increasingly difficult decisions today" [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt]. He also says it "won’t fix the long-term issues until
  we overhaul the whole education funding system" [SRC:legislature/social/2026-11-19_ruiz-instagram-osba-panel.txt]. The post gives no dollar amount and names no bill.
- Feb 2026: told KATU that discussions about possibly tapping reserves were ongoing and lawmakers would keep
  monitoring (KATU paraphrase, no direct quote) [NEWS:NEWS-20260217-katu-1]. The fund was not tapped in the 2026 session
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1].

## Analysis
- The JPEA's sufficiency report, under his co-chairmanship, says 2025-27 met the QEM. It compares 2025-27 funding with
  the 2024 QEM; the 2026 QEM (status quo $13.78B for 2027-29) sets a new bar, so the 2025-27 finding doesn't carry
  into 2027-29 (see `decisions.md#ssf-2027-29`).
- His Nov 2026 reserve proposal goes further than his Feb 2026 stance (per KATU's paraphrase, keep monitoring). It is
  narrower than PPS's request for a $500M statewide PERS buy-down [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]: targeted at districts under strain, especially those that have already
  used their own reserves. He presents it as one-time money while a new funding system is built, which speaks to
  the objection that reserves shouldn't pay ongoing costs, though PERS payments are themselves an ongoing cost.
- He wants a different yardstick for "adequate," not necessarily less money. Open question: what level he'd fund to
  in 2027-29 while the QEM is under review.
