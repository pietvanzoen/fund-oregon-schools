# Sen. Kate Lieber

Official page: <https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/lieber>

Sen. Kate Lieber is a Democrat representing Senate District 14 (Beaverton and Southwest Portland)
[SRC:legislature/members/lieber.html]. She is Senate co-chair of the Joint Committee on Ways and Means, the committee
that writes the state budget, including the State School Fund
[SRC:legislature/appointments/2026_senate-committee-assignments.pdf p.7]. Senate Democrats chose her again as Ways and
Means co-chair for the 2027 session on Nov. 13, 2026 [SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-13_senate-dems-2027-leadership.pdf p.1].
She is the only budget writer already named for a 2027 role (a caucus pick; the formal appointment comes when
the session organizes).

## Role in K-12 budget decisions
- Co-chaired the 2025-27 budget: the co-chairs' March 2025 framework proposed $11.4 billion for the State School Fund, assuming
  oversight and accountability measures were adopted [SRC:fiscal/lfo/2025-03_co-chair-budget-framework.pdf p.2]
  [SRC:legislature/press/2025-03-19_jwm-framework.pdf p.1].
- On the full Ways and Means Committee she voted yes on Senate Bill 141's budget [SRC:legislature/bills/2025R1_SB141_budget-report.pdf p.1].
- Co-led the 2026 rebalance after what the co-chairs call federal budget cuts; the co-chairs say it maintains critical state services
  [SRC:legislature/press/2026-03-01_jwm-rebalance.pdf p.1].

## On revenue and the budget
- On the 2025-27 framework: "This framework is a starting point" [SRC:legislature/press/2025-03-19_jwm-framework.pdf p.1].
- On the 2026 rebalance: "The federal budget cuts have caused both short-term and long-term crises for Oregon's
  budget" [SRC:legislature/press/2026-03-01_jwm-rebalance.pdf p.1].
- Same release: the rebalance "preserves essential state programs for the next 18 months to give us time to solve
  the larger challenges the federal budget cuts will have for Oregon" [SRC:legislature/press/2026-03-01_jwm-rebalance.pdf p.1].
- At a late-February 2026 media briefing: said the general fund will need long-term fixes to sustainable revenue
  streams (reported speech, not a direct quote) [NEWS:NEWS-20260301-opb-1].

## On the Education Stability Fund
- At a May 7, 2026, school funding forum (Community + Parents for Public Schools), she said the Legislature might have looked to
  the fund in the 2026 short session had it been forced to cut school funding below the current service level; it
  wasn't, so it didn't [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1].
- Same forum: "If you use one-time money to pay for ongoing expenses, then you're still going to have a cliff…"
  [NEWS:NEWS-20260510-wweek-1]
- Analysis: her stated test, as Willamette Week reported it, is whether school funding would be cut below the current service level (the amount
  needed to continue existing authorized programs), not whether districts face deficits. The 2026 Quality Education Model puts the cost of today's
  services about $1.2 billion above the State School Fund's current service level, to come from state funds outside
  the fund (see `topics/quality-education-model.md`), so a State School Fund at the current service level passes her
  test whether or not those other funds keep pace. Gov. Tina Kotek has described a similar objection, from unnamed
  legislative leaders, as one she can understand (see `kotek.md`).
