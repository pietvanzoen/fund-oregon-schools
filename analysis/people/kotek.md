# Gov. Tina Kotek

Tina Kotek is governor of Oregon; she was reelected in November 2026 [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1]. She submits the
Governor's Recommended Budget for 2027-29 in December [NEWS:NEWS-2026-salemreporter-1].

## On school funding
- Proposed $11.36 billion for the State School Fund for 2025-27, an 11% increase over the $10.2 billion for 2023-25
  [NEWS:NEWS-20241203-osba-1].
- Proposed raising the fund's 2025-27 current service level (the amount needed to continue existing authorized
  programs) by about $515 million over the original calculation [SRC:gov/press/2024_ssf-csl-proposal.html].
- Said in April 2026 she didn't expect extra school money beyond the current budget [NEWS:NEWS-20260430-wweek-1].
- After reelection, speaking about revenue proposals such as a wealth tax, said she had made no commitments on how to
  pay for things [NEWS:NEWS-20261106-occ-1].
- On her 2027-29 budget: "my budget will be prioritizing education and particularly summer investments again"
  [NEWS:NEWS-2026-salemreporter-1].
- On the December 2026 revenue forecast, released Nov. 18, 2026: "Today’s revenue forecast shows Oregon state
  government has steady financial footing on which to protect essential services, even as stubborn inflation persists
  as a result of tepid federal action." The statement does not mention schools or reserves
  [SRC:gov/press/2026-11-18_kotek_revenue-forecast-statement.html].

## On school outcomes (2026 platform and actions)
- Her September 2026 platform: instructional time at the national average by the end of a second term; fewer student
  absences; tougher diploma requirements; less access to social media on school-issued devices; and, under
  affordability, universal preschool [NEWS:NEWS-20260917-opb-1].
- Executive Order 26-06 (signed April 15, 2026 [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_preserving-instructional-time.pdf p.3]) barring further cuts to instructional time and requiring districts that
  cut it for 2025-26 or 2026-27 to submit a plan to restore 2024-25 levels no later than the start of 2027-28 [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_preserving-instructional-time.pdf p.2].
- The executive order's FAQ asks "Is this just another 'unfunded mandate' for districts?" and answers only "No."
  [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.3]. For funding it says she directed the Oregon Department of Education "to explore potential options within the
  existing budget" [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2].
- The same FAQ: "the Governor's Office recognizes these dollars may not be sufficient for some school districts to
  cover increasing costs" [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2].
- Introduced Senate Bill 141, the 2025 Education Accountability Act [SRC:ode/accountability/2025-12_sb141-progress-report.pdf p.5].

## On the funding formula
- Analysis: Kotek has spoken about the formula, but as of Nov. 22, 2026, the archive has no bill, budget item or
  formula design from her that replaces or changes enrollment as its basis. Her Nov. 6 remarks float "student need"
  as a possible basis; they are not a proposal.
- At her Nov. 6, 2026, press conference, as quoted by KATU: "Maybe enrollment is the wrong marker. That was
  established almost 30 years ago. Maybe it has to be student need" [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1]. She added
  that rethinking it "is a longer conversation than what is happening in districts right now"
  [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1].
- The press conference video has not been archived or checked yet.

## On online schools
- In a statement to the Oregon Journalism Project, published Nov. 3, 2026, Kotek said she "is actively reviewing the
  state's current approach to virtual education" and "is concerned about how public dollars are distributed and what
  accountability measures are in place" [NEWS:NEWS-20261103-wweek-1].
- Analysis: as of Nov. 20, 2026, no proposal from her on virtual schools is in the archive; see `topics/virtual-charters.md`.

## On the Education Stability Fund
- Using it requires a three-fifths vote in each chamber plus either an economic trigger or a governor's emergency
  declaration [SRC:fiscal/lro/2025-11_reserve-funds.pdf p.1] (OPB described it as an emergency declaration and a
  three-fifths vote [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1]).
- Asked by OPB in October 2026 about past calls to use the fund for schools, she said "That is up to the
  legislature" and, of raising it with legislative leaders, "I would bring it up, and it was summarily dismissed".
  She described their argument, which she said she could understand, that "if you use one-time dollars, which is
  what a reserve fund is, that will have a greater cliff down the line" [NEWS:NEWS-20261102-opb-1].
- The April 2026 FAQ for Executive Order 26-06: she "remains open to discussing all options to support districts," but does not plan
  to declare an emergency and call a special session for the fund unless legislative leaders indicate a three-fifths
  majority would approve it [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.2] [SRC:gov/eo/eo-26-06_faq.pdf p.3]. (KTVZ described
  this as openness to tapping the fund [NEWS:NEWS-20260416-ktvz-1]; the FAQ itself is more guarded.)
- Nov. 6, 2026, press conference: KATU reports she does not support using one-time state reserves for Portland Public
  Schools' budget problems [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1].
- Analysis: the April FAQ is conditional (no special session for the fund at that time unless a supermajority is
  lined up); the November statement, as KATU reported it, sets her against one-time reserves for Portland Public Schools'
  budget problems. On one-time money for ongoing costs, the archive has her describing unnamed legislative leaders'
  objection as one she can understand (October 2026), framed as their argument.

## On Portland Public Schools closures
- Oct. 13, 2026, letter to Portland Public Schools Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong and School Board Chair Eddie Wang [SRC:gov/letters/2026-10-13_kotek-letter-to-pps-school-closures.pdf p.1]:
  - Said her office had heard from parents, students and neighbors expressing shock and disappointment, and
    confusion about the timeline and why schools were picked [SRC:gov/letters/2026-10-13_kotek-letter-to-pps-school-closures.pdf p.1].
  - "the district must take the necessary time for families to understand and adequately weigh in before
    decisions are made" [SRC:gov/letters/2026-10-13_kotek-letter-to-pps-school-closures.pdf p.1].
  - "Taking the time to get this right is not a delay in making difficult decisions, but rather an essential part
    of the commitment to community partnership." [SRC:gov/letters/2026-10-13_kotek-letter-to-pps-school-closures.pdf p.1]
  - Said every family deserves clear, transparent information on the data, the factors considered and the
    tradeoffs [SRC:gov/letters/2026-10-13_kotek-letter-to-pps-school-closures.pdf p.1].
  - Analysis: the letter is about process. It asks the district to slow down for engagement but does not ask it to withdraw its
    plans, sets no timeline, and says nothing about state funding, the State School Fund or reserves [SRC:gov/letters/2026-10-13_kotek-letter-to-pps-school-closures.pdf p.1]. OPB's account
    [NEWS:NEWS-20261014-opb-1] is consistent with the text.
- "Portland needs to do everything it possibly can to cut administrative costs and other things" before it
  talks about closing schools (the end of the sentence is KATU's paraphrase) [NEWS:NEWS-2026-katu-kotek-pps-1]
