# How Oregon school funding works

The Legislature sets the State School Fund in each two-year budget and shares it among school districts by a formula
in state law [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. This guide takes the money for day-to-day operations step by step,
from where it comes from to who decides, and links each step to the page that holds the documents behind it.

<!-- site: visual dollar-to-school -->

## 1. Where the money comes from {#where-the-money-comes-from}

The state pays about 70% of the cost of K-12 schools, according to the Quality Education Commission's 2026 report
[SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.104]. Of the money the funding formula shares out, state money is about two-thirds
and local revenue, mostly property taxes, is the rest
[SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/293759_ssf-budget-review.pdf p.1]. The state's part of the State School Fund is General
Fund money, from the state's main budget account, along with Lottery Funds, marijuana tax revenue and Corporate
Activity Tax revenue [SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/293759_ssf-budget-review.pdf p.1].

Before 1990 the shares were the other way round: districts raised about 70% of their operating money locally and the
state about 30% [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.104]. The change followed the property tax limits voters passed in
the 1990s, which are step 4 [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.104]. The archive doesn't yet have a source on how
federal money outside the formula reaches districts.

## 2. How the state decides how much {#how-the-state-decides-how-much}

The Legislature sets the State School Fund amount in each two-year budget; for 2025-27 it adopted $11.36 billion
[SRC:fiscal/lfo/2025-07_lab-summary-2025-27.pdf p.1]. The yardstick for the next budget is the State School
Fund's current service level, the amount needed to continue existing programs [SRC:gov/press/2024_ssf-csl-proposal.html].
The Department of Administrative Services works it out by adjusting the last budget's State School Fund spending for
inflation and enrollment, and puts it at $12.56 billion [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.100]; that's the 2027-29
figure available when the Quality Education Commission's 2026 report was published
[SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103].

The Quality Education Commission makes a separate estimate of what schools need, which counts other state money as
well as the State School Fund, and leaves what counts as adequate to the Legislature
[SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]:

## 3. How it's shared among districts {#how-its-shared-among-districts}

Once the Legislature sets the amount, the formula in chapter 327 of the Oregon Revised Statutes divides the State
School Fund among districts [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. Each district's amount is its General Purpose Grant, the
main part, plus transportation and high-cost disability grants, minus its local revenue, which is mostly property taxes
[SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html] [SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/293759_ssf-budget-review.pdf p.1]:

## 4. What districts can add locally, and the limits {#what-districts-can-add-locally-and-the-limits}

A district can add operating money only through a local option levy, a tax its voters approve on top of its permanent
tax rate [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]. Measure 50 put those permanent rates in
place of most earlier levies [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.3]. Up to a cap, levy money
isn't subtracted from the district's State School Fund grant; its regular property taxes are
[SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. Measure 5 required the state to replace schools' lost property tax through 1996, but
the Department of Revenue says that had little effect, because the Legislature appropriated more than required
[SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]:

## 5. What the money is supposed to buy {#what-the-money-is-supposed-to-buy}

State law sets purposes for some grants, and the State Board of Education sets targets for student results; the
archive has little yet on what the money buys. Some state money comes as grants paid in addition to the State School
Fund, with purposes set in law [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. The largest in 2025-27, by the count of the
Legislature's Joint Committee on Public Education Appropriation, was the Student Investment Account
[SRC:legislature/olis/2025I1/310959_jpea-adequacy-report.pdf p.29]:

## 6. Who decides, and when {#who-decides-and-when}

The Governor's recommended budget is due Dec. 1, 2026 [SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html]. In the Legislature, the
Joint Committee on Ways and Means and its Education Subcommittee take up the State School Fund budget bill
[SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/307212_sb5516-ssf-work-session.pdf p.1]. Who holds each role is on `people/people.csv`,
and what's being decided now, with each option and where it stands, is on `decisions.md`.
