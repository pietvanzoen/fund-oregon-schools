# How Oregon school funding works

The Legislature sets the State School Fund in each two-year budget and shares it among school districts by a formula
in state law [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. This guide takes the money for day-to-day operations step by step,
from where it comes from to who decides, and links each step to the page that holds the documents behind it.

<!-- site: visual dollar-to-school -->

## 1. Where the money comes from {#where-the-money-comes-from}

Of the money the funding formula shares among districts in 2025-27, state money is about two-thirds and local
revenue, mostly property taxes, is the rest, according to the Legislative Fiscal Office
[SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/293759_ssf-budget-review.pdf p.1]. The state's part of the State School Fund is General
Fund money, from the state's main budget account, along with Lottery Funds, marijuana tax revenue and Corporate
Activity Tax revenue [SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/293759_ssf-budget-review.pdf p.1].

The state's share of school operating money grew in the 1990s, as voters limited property taxes, according to the
Oregon Encyclopedia [SRC:reference/oregon-encyclopedia_measure-5.html]; step 4 covers those limits. The archive
doesn't yet have a full split of today's school money between the state, local taxes and the federal government, or
a source on how federal money outside the formula reaches districts.

## 2. How the state decides how much {#how-the-state-decides-how-much}

The Legislature sets the State School Fund amount in each two-year budget; for 2025-27 it adopted $11.36 billion
[SRC:fiscal/lfo/2025-07_lab-summary-2025-27.pdf p.1]. The baseline the archive compares 2027-29 proposals against is
the State School Fund's current service level, the amount needed to continue existing programs [SRC:gov/press/2024_ssf-csl-proposal.html].
The Department of Administrative Services works it out by adjusting the last budget's State School Fund spending for
inflation and enrollment, and put the 2027-29 figure at $12.56 billion as of the Quality Education Commission's 2026
report [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.100] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117].

The Quality Education Commission makes a separate estimate of what schools need, which counts other state money as
well as the State School Fund, and leaves what counts as adequate to the Legislature
[SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103] [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]:

## 3. How it's shared among districts {#how-its-shared-among-districts}

Once the Legislature sets the amount, the formula in chapter 327 of the Oregon Revised Statutes divides the State
School Fund among districts [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. Each district's amount is its General Purpose Grant plus transportation and high-cost disability grants, minus its local revenue, which is mostly property taxes
[SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html] [SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/293759_ssf-budget-review.pdf p.1]:

## 4. What districts can add locally, and the limits {#what-districts-can-add-locally-and-the-limits}

Beyond its permanent tax rate, a district can raise more property tax for operations only through a local option levy,
which its voters approve [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]. Measure 50, passed by
voters in 1997, put permanent tax rates in place of most earlier levies
[SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.3]. Up to a cap, levy money
isn't subtracted from the district's State School Fund grant; its regular property taxes are
[SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. Measure 5, passed by voters in 1990 to cap property taxes,
required the state to replace schools' lost property tax through 1996, but the Department of Revenue, in a 2009
history, said that had little effect, because the Legislature appropriated more than required
[SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]:

## 5. What the money is supposed to buy {#what-the-money-is-supposed-to-buy}

The archive has little yet on what the money buys. Some state money comes as grants paid in addition to the State School
Fund, with purposes set in law [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. Of the state grants the Legislature's Joint
Committee on Public Education Appropriation counted as core K-12 funding for 2025-27, the largest is the Student
Investment Account

## 6. Who decides, and when {#who-decides-and-when}

The Governor's recommended budget is due Dec. 1, 2026 [SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html]. In 2025, the Education
Subcommittee of the Legislature's Joint Committee on Ways and Means took up the State School Fund budget bill, Senate
Bill 5516 [SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/307212_sb5516-ssf-work-session.pdf p.1]. Who holds each role is on `people/people.csv`,
and what's being decided now, with each option and where it stands, is on `decisions.md`.
