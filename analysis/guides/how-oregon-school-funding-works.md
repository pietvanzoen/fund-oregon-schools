# How Oregon school funding works

This guide follows a school district's money for day-to-day operations, from state taxes to the district, in six
steps. Each step says how one part works and links to the page that holds the documents behind it.

<!-- site: visual dollar-to-school -->

## 1. Where the money comes from {#where-the-money-comes-from}

A district's money for day-to-day operations comes from the state, from local property taxes and levies, and from the
federal government [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. Most of the State School Fund is General Fund money that the
Legislature appropriates, and part of it is Lottery Funds [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]
[SRC:legislature/olis/2025R1/293759_ssf-budget-review.pdf p.1].

The state's share grew after voters limited property taxes in the 1990s [SRC:reference/oregon-encyclopedia_measure-5.html]. Measure 5 required the state to replace
schools' lost property tax through 1996, and Measure 50 made replacing school revenue lost to Measure 5's limits a
constitutional requirement [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4]. Step 4 covers those
limits. The archive doesn't yet have today's shares from the state,
local taxes and the federal government, or a source on how federal money outside the formula reaches districts.

## 2. How the state decides how much {#how-the-state-decides-how-much}

The State School Fund is the primary source of state funding for K-12 public education
[SRC:gov/press/2024_ssf-csl-proposal.html]. The Legislature adopted $11.36 billion for 2025-27
[SRC:fiscal/lfo/2025-07_lab-summary-2025-27.pdf p.1]. Budget writers start the next budget from the current service
level, the amount needed to continue existing programs [SRC:gov/press/2024_ssf-csl-proposal.html]: $12.56 billion for
2027-29, as estimated by the Department of Administrative Services [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.103].

The Quality Education Commission makes a separate estimate of what schools need, and leaves what counts as adequate
to the Legislature [SRC:ode/qec/2026_qem_report.pdf p.117]:

## 3. How it's shared among districts {#how-its-shared-among-districts}

Once the Legislature sets the amount, the formula in chapter 327 of the Oregon Revised Statutes divides the State
School Fund among districts [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. Each district gets general purpose, transportation and
high-cost disability grants, minus its local revenue [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]:

## 4. What districts can add locally, and the limits {#what-districts-can-add-locally-and-the-limits}

The property taxes a district collects stay with it, and are subtracted from its State School Fund grant
[SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]. A local option levy is the only way to raise operating money above a district's
permanent tax rate [SRC:fiscal/dor/2009-06_brief-history-property-taxation.pdf p.4], and up to a cap it isn't
subtracted from the grant [SRC:legislature/ors/ors327.html]:

## 6. Who decides, and when {#who-decides-and-when}

The Governor's recommended budget is due Dec. 1, 2026 [SRC:legislature/ors/ors291.html], and the Legislature's Joint
Committee on Ways and Means writes the budget the Legislature votes on
[SRC:legislature/press/2026-11-13_senate-dems-2027-leadership.pdf p.1]. Who holds each role is on
`people/people.csv`, and what's being decided now, with each option and where it stands, is on `decisions.md`.
