Dear Superintendent Armstrong and Chair Wang,
I recognize the decisions about school building closures are among the most difficult a school district
can face. As you know, these decisions do not just concern the physical spaces where students learn.
These decisions are informed by enrollment data and budget pressures, but they are personal for
students, families, educators and surrounding communities. Neighborhood schools are often the heart of
our communities, providing a sense of belonging and connection that extends beyond the classroom.
Since the options for potential closures were released by your district, my office has heard from parents,
students, and neighbors expressing shock and disappointment about the possibility of losing a
neighborhood school and confusion about the decision-making timeline and why schools were identified
for change or closure.
As you know, any decision that could impact student success cannot be taken lightly, and while leaders
making tough decisions must always grapple with grief and frustration, confusion about the process is
not something that we should accept. That is why the district must take the necessary time for families
to understand and adequately weigh in before decisions are made.
Every family deserves access to clear, transparent information about the data that informs these
decisions, the factors considered, and the hard tradeoffs that must be made to make sure Oregon students
have the best opportunity to succeed.
I urge you to take the time to work through these challenging discussions with transparency,
accessibility, and listening through authentic engagement. Taking the time to get this right is not a delay
in making difficult decisions, but rather an essential part of the commitment to community partnership.
Oregon’s students, families, communities, and educators deserve nothing less.
Sincerely,

Governor Tina Kotek
