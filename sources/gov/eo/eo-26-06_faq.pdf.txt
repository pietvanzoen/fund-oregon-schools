EO26-06 FAQ
As the Superintendent of Public Instruction for the State of Oregon, Governor Tina Kotek is
steadfast in her commitment to ensure that all Oregon students have access to sufficient and
high-quality instructional time to support their success in school.

What does Executive Order 26-06 do?
•

•

It calls on the State Board of Education (SBE) to establish temporary rules to preserve
current student instructional hours by prohibiting the reduction of time students are in
direct contact with teachers during the school day.
It directs the Oregon Department of Education (ODE) to work with any school district that
has already reduced student instructional time in the 2025-26 and/or 2026-27 school
years to develop a plan for how they will recoup the reduction in student instructional
time by the start of the 2027-28 school year.

Does the EO apply only to districts or are charter schools also included?
The EO applies to districts and charter schools, entities that were already subject to instructional
time requirements under the existing OAR 581-022-2320.

Why does the EO affect existing rules about whether professional development
and parent-teacher conference time can count as instructional time?
EO 26-06 refocuses time on classroom learning to reflect actual student-peer-teacher
engagement. This includes eliminating the practice of counting certain non-classroom activities,
such as professional development and parent-teacher conferences, toward required hours of
instructional time. Defining student instructional time as contact time with students in schools
will improve students’ ability to succeed. The Governor values professional development and
parent-teacher conference time and supports that work continuing outside of the time where
students and teachers interact. Implementing the EO through the rule-making process will allow
the State to consider the challenges school districts are facing while simultaneously holding the
value of consistent time in schools with students, teachers, and peers.

Why did the Governor issue the EO now, while districts are in the midst of budget
planning for the 2026-27 school year? How might this order affect district
decisions about staffing, class sizes, and/or school closures?
Taking this action now is important so that districts can plan for the future in ways that do not
impact or reduce student instructional time. Finding solutions to the many challenges districts
are facing cannot come at a cost of less student instructional time.

What are the options for emergency funding to support this EO?
Given districts are currently budgeting for the 2026-27 school year, the EO was developed with
a directive to recoup reduced student instructional time by the 2027-28 school year. Governor
Kotek has directed ODE to explore potential options within the existing budget to support
districts and provide technical support.

Is the Governor opposed to accessing the Education Stability Fund mid-biennium
to support districts struggling financially?
The Education Stability Fund (ESF) was conceived as one-time funding available for education
when economic emergencies arise that result in statewide General Fund reductions, specifically
initiating financial cuts to the State School Fund (SSF). While many districts are navigating midbiennium financial challenges and budget reductions, the State has not reduced the SSF in the
2025-27 biennium. The SSF increased 11.4% as a result of legislation in 2025, but the
Governor’s Office recognizes these dollars may not be sufficient for some school districts to
cover increasing costs.

Is this just another “unfunded mandate” for districts?
No.

Are school districts that have already built furlough days into their 2025-26 and/or
2026-27 school year exempt from this EO?
No, districts that have implemented furlough days this school year and/or next school year are
not exempt from the requirements of the EO. These districts will need to submit a plan to ODE
on how they will recoup reduced student instructional time starting in the 2027-28 school year.

If a school district had scheduled furlough days in 2025-26, but the district still
met or exceeded the minimum hours of instructional time required by Oregon
Administrative Rules, are they still required to take corrective action to make up
that time?
Yes. If any student contact time was reduced in the 2025-26 year from the prior year, the district
must take corrective action, even if it still met the minimum instructional hour requirements.
Districts are required to develop a plan to make up for any student contact lost time.

What specific school districts will be impacted by this EO?
The Governor’s Office and ODE are ready to work alongside school districts that are impacted
by the EO and will take a hands-on approach to ensuring the continued success of the students
and faculty in those districts.
