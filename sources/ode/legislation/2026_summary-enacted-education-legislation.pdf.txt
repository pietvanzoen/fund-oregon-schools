SUMMARY
Final business for the 2026 Legislative Session concluded on March 6. The House and Senate
chambers first convened on February 2, and finished up with time to spare from the
Constitutionally required Sine Die date of March 8. The Legislature tackled many issues in this
short session, with a particular focus on revenue impacts to the state’s budget and response to
federal policy concerns.
The Oregon Department of Education’s legislative agenda concentrated on a technical change
for the Military Interstate Children’s Compact, often referred to as MIC3. The MIC3 is an
agreement among all 50 states and the District of Columbia to ensure military-connected
students face minimal disruption in education when military families relocate across state lines.
It addresses key transition issues such as enrollment, placement, attendance, eligibility for
programs and activities, graduation requirements, and transfer of records.
Oregon first adopted the Compact in 2014, and ODE continues to hold responsibility for
supporting the implementation of the compact and representing Oregon nationally. Our ask,
contained in HB 4066, allowed Oregon’s statute to fully align with the model language used by
other states. The bill passed both chambers, ensuring consistent protections for military
students as intended when the Compact was adopted.
While this short session was a quiet one for our legislative agenda, the systems-changing work
continues at the Department internally, as outlined in Senate Bill 141 (2025). The 2025
Education Accountability Act reflects a significant step forward in how we ensure every Oregon
student has access to meaningful learning opportunities. At ODE, we’re streamlining reporting
and introducing policies to help schools and districts meaningfully improve how we as a state
serve Oregon’s students, families, and communities. This work is about building trust, driving
equity, and ensuring that every student has a real opportunity to succeed and thrive.

A special thank you to all the legislative coordinators, bill managers and the fiscal team within ODE.

MOVING EDUCATION EQUITY FORWARD
HB 4149 – Relating to homeless students
House Vote: Carried by Levy E. Passed. Ayes, 39; Nays, 3; Excused, 18.
Senate Vote: Carried by Neron Misslin. Passed. Ayes, 26; Nays, 2; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4149 enacts existing federal requirements for the education of homeless students into state
law.

SB 1533 – Relating to the rights of children in care
House Vote: Carried by Elmer. Passed. Ayes, 42; Nays, 1; Absent, 6; Excused, 11.
Senate Vote: Carried by Gelser Blouin. Passed. Ayes, 30.
Senate Bill 1533 modifies the Foster Children’s Bill of Rights, to include rights such as the right to
regularly attend school and have access to a free and appropriate public education.

SB 1538 – Relating to conduct of public education
House Vote: Carried by Rieke Smith. Passed. Ayes, 36; Nays, 21; Excused, 3.
Senate Vote: Carried by Frederick. Passed. Ayes, 23; Nays, 7.
Senate Bill 1538 guarantees admission to Oregon school districts’ instructional programs, requires the
Oregon Department of Justice (DOJ) to modify the model policies it has created for school districts, and
requires school districts to distribute those model policies to their employees.

SB 1594 – Relating to immigration
House Vote: Carried by Ruiz. Passed. Ayes, 33; Nays, 14; Absent, 6; Excused, 7.
Senate Vote: Carried by Prozanski. Passed. Ayes, 18; Nays, 11; Excused, 1.
Senate Bill 1594 directs the Oregon Department of Justice (DOJ) to consult with the Office of Immigrant
and Refugee Advancement (OIRA) during its development, review, or update of model policies
concerning immigration enforcement, pursuant to ORS 180.810. The measure also resolves a conflict
with Senate Bill 1538 A (2026).

EDUCATION OVERSIGHT
HB 4066 – Relating to education
House Vote: Carried by Hudson. Passed. Ayes, 40; Nays, 13--Boice, Boshart Davis, Bunch, Cate, Edwards,
Elmer, Harbick, Helfrich, Lewis, Osborne, Reschke, Skarlatos, Yunker; Excused, 7.
Senate Vote: Carried by Sollman. Passed. Ayes, 28; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4066 makes a series of changes to Oregon education laws, including updating references to
federal statute in the Military Interstate Children’s Compact, allowing Education Service District (ESD)
boards to offer board members a stipend of up to $500 per month, extending the date by which ESDs
must comply with state light bulb requirements, granting rulemaking authority to the State Board of
Education in areas of statute that incorrectly grant authority to ODE, and exempting school districts

HB 4154 – Relating to student attendance data
House Vote: Carried by Wise. Passed. Ayes, 40; Nays, 1; Excused, 19.
Senate Vote: Carried by Weber. Passed. Ayes, 26; Nays, 3; Excused, 1.
House Bill 4154 requires ODE to use existing average daily membership (ADM) reports to publish

EMPLOYMENT
HB 4013 – Relating to labor standards
House Vote: Carried by Grayber. Passed. Ayes, 36; Nays, 22; Excused, 2.
Senate Vote: Carried by Taylor. Passed. Ayes, 18; Nays, 11; Excused, 1.
House Bill 4013 establishes that Oregon’s rules on the total hours a minor may work may not be less
restrictive than the requirements of the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) that are in effect on
January 1, 2026. It permits the Oregon Bureau of Labor and Industries (BOLI) to adopt rules to conform
to changes in the FLSA or Oregon law that increase protections for minors.

HB 4089 – Relating to criminal offenses related to work
House Vote: Carried by Grayber. Passed. Ayes, 33; Nays, 9; Excused, 18.
Senate Vote: Carried by Neron Misslin. Passed. Ayes, 26; Nays, 2; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4089 modifies when a person commits the crime of theft of services.

HB 4111 – Relating to immigration-related practices
House Vote: Carried by Chotzen. Passed. Ayes, 34; Nays, 19; Excused, 7.
Senate Vote: Carried by Broadman, Prozanski. Passed. Ayes, 20; Nays, 9; Excused, 1.
House Bill 4111 prohibits an employer from taking adverse action against an employee for updating
personal information to reflect a lawful change to their federal employment authorization. It states that
it is legal for an employer to comply with federal employment authorization verification requirements.
The measure protects employers if a third-party benefits administrator independently takes adverse
action in response to changes in the employee’s personal information or federal employment
authorizations.

SB 1527 – Relating to health care
House Vote: Carried by Levy E. Passed. Ayes, 54; Excused, 6.
Carried by Hayden, Patterson. Passed. Ayes, 29; Excused, 1.
Senate Bill 1527 requires group and individual health insurance plans, including the Public Employees’
Benefit Board (PEBB) and the Oregon Educators Benefit Board (OEBB), to fully cover the cost of medically
necessary cervical cancer screenings.

WORKFORCE DEVELOPMENT
SB 1512 – Relating to workforce development
House Vote: Carried by Fragala. Passed. Ayes, 36; Nays, 16; Excused, 8.
Senate Vote: Carried by Pham. Passed. Ayes, 28; Nays, 2.
Senate Bill 1512 extends the Prosperity 10,000 program and modifies its goals and purposes, including
requiring local boards to consider how entities’ grant funding proposals demonstrate engagement with
priority populations in targeted industry sectors, including collaboration with industry and education
partners, and prioritizing the use of federal and private funds.

HB 4075 – Relating to hospital finance
Carried by Wright, Nosse. Passed. Ayes, 42; Nays, 15; Excused, 3.
Senate Vote: Carried by Smith DB. Passed. Ayes, 16; Nays, 12; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4075 directs the State Treasurer to use funds from the Unclaimed Property and Estates Fund
to guarantee a rural hospital stabilization loan to a hospital that meets specific criteria. The Oregon
State Treasury holds unclaimed property in trust in the Common School Fund until it is claimed by its
owners. Funds are invested by the state, and earned interest is distributed biannually across Oregon’s
197 public school districts.

budget. This includes changing the reporting structure for ODE on the Summer Learning Grant Program,

HEALTH AND SAFETY
HB 4079 – Relating to policies for when federal immigration authorities enter public
education property
House Vote: Carried by McDonald. Passed. Ayes, 35; Nays, 22; Excused, 3.
Senate Vote: Carried by Neron Misslin. Passed. Ayes, 18; Nays, 10; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4079 requires school districts and institutions of higher education to adopt policies governing
institutional responses to federal immigration authorities on school property.

HB 4160 – Relating to procedures for medical emergencies in schools
House Vote: Carried by Yunker. Passed. Ayes, 53; Nays, 1; Excused, 6.
Senate Vote: Carried by Robinson. Passed. Ayes, 28; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4160 requires schools to have a cardiac emergency response plan for responding to medical
emergencies. It establishes requirements for the response plan, including alignment with national
standards, as well as the placement, identification, and maintenance of automated external
defibrillators (AEDs). It requires athletic coaches and other staff to be trained in cardiopulmonary
resuscitation (CPR) and the use of AEDs.

SB 1504 – Relating to the administration of epinephrine
House Vote: Carried by Nguyen D. Passed. Ayes, 56; Excused, 4.
Senate Vote: Carried by President Wagner. Passed. Ayes, 30.
Senate Bill 1504 allows students and school staff to administer a premeasured dose of epinephrine via
auto-injector, nasal spray, or other method.

HB 4088 – Relating to health
House Vote: Carried by Fragala. Passed. Ayes, 34; Nays, 22; Excused, 4.
Senate Vote: Carried by Gelser Blouin, Reynolds. Passed. Ayes, 18; Nays, 12.
House Bill 4088 creates policies regarding legal protections for legal reproductive or gender-affirming
health care activities that occur in Oregon. The measure relates to gubernatorial actions, data privacy,
court records, public records, and midwifery practice. Under the measure, the public records that are
exempt from disclosure will include personally identifiable information relating to applications for or
receipt of legal reproductive or gender-affirming health care activities and applications for legal sex
changes.

HB 4121 – Relating to emergency management
House Vote: Carried by Evans. Passed. Ayes, 54; Nays, 1; Excused, 5.
Senate Vote: Carried by Manning Jr. Passed. Ayes, 26; Nays, 2; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4121 creates statewide emergency preparedness offices and authorities to coordinate
emergency management.

HB 4138 – Relating to limitations on the actions of public employees
House Vote: Carried by Kropf. Passed. Ayes, 34; Nays, 18; Excused, 8.
Senate Vote: Carried by Campos. Passed. Ayes, 18; Nays, 10; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4138 prohibits employees of a state or local law enforcement agency or public body from
intentionally assisting a federal or out-of-state law enforcement agency in investigating, apprehending,
or arresting people if those activities are being done:
• On the basis of a person’s exercise of constitutional rights related to speech, expression,
association, or assembly
• On the basis of a person’s membership in a protected class
• As part of an unlawful search or seizure or other unconstitutional surveillance activity

HB 4161 – Relating to use of official position or office
House Vote: Carried by Sosa. Passed. Ayes, 51; Excused, 9.
Senate Vote: Carried by Golden. Passed. Ayes, 28; Excused, 2
House Bill 4161 provides exceptions to the prohibited use by a public official of official position or office
to obtain financial gain or avoid financial detriment that permit a public body to give public officials
food, beverages and merchandise.

HB 4178 – Relating to rounding procedures in transactions

TEACHING AND LEARNING
HB 4022 – Relating to children’s literacy
House Vote: Carried by Bowman. Passed. Ayes, 53; Nays, 4; Excused, 3.
Senate Vote: Carried by Patterson. Passed. Ayes, 27; Nays, 1; Excused, 2.
House Bill 4022 establishes the Oregon Imagination Library program to provide a free book each month
to eligible children. It directs the Department of Early Learning and Care (DELC) to implement and
administer the program.
