Testimony to the Joint Committee on Public Education
Appropriation on the Committee’s Final Report
December 18, 2023

Co-Chairs Frederick and McLain,
Committee Members,
Oregon PTA deeply appreciates the challenging work of the Joint Committee on Public Education
Appropriation and of the Quality Education Commission, and the detailed and thoughtful
discussion this committee has had over the last few months.
We agree with the conclusion of the committee’s report that “state funding appropriated for the
2023-2025 biennium for K-12 public education is insufficient.” 1
As Oregon PTA we urge you:
● to support a thorough update to the Quality Education Model to reflect the current
challenges and opportunities we see in our schools;
● to work with the Governor’s office and all educational stakeholders on reviewing the
education budget process;
● and, ultimately, to re-prioritize providing high quality education to our children in
allocations from the General Fund.
As our members can attest, our students, our teachers, our schools are struggling. You have heard
about overcrowded classrooms in Portland Public Schools; you have heard about the painful cuts in
Salem-Keizer; about school buildings with unresolved maintenance issues; the challenges in our
rural districts. We are certain you hear it from parents in your own districts.
Beyond the lived experience of our members, the state’s own data shows that our student’s
struggles are serious:
● Our absentee rate is among the nation’s highest.2
● Test results, across the board, are “bleak”.3

One in five of our eleventh graders does not feel safe at school.4
One in four of our Black high school students is not on track to graduate.5
Asked about their experience in the last 30 days, a full quarter of our eighth graders
reported having been bullied at school.6

And most distressingly, in 2022, one in seven of our high school juniors seriously contemplated
committing suicide.7
We are failing our children. We are not coming close to meeting their educational nor their
social-emotional needs. We are not, as the report seems to imply, 11% away from quality education
in Oregon.8 The gap is much higher. It will take significant investments to achieve the Education
Quality Goals listed in statute and in the committee report.
Against this backdrop, it is disheartening, as figure 8 on page 19 of your report shows, that the
legislature has recently cut the share of General and Lottery Fund dollars going towards K-12
education - from about 38% in 2017 to only 28% for this biennium.
As a share of state General Fund and Lottery dollars, Oregon has seemingly not spent this little on
public schools in any biennium since 1991.9
In absolute dollar terms, even the new resources from the Student Success Act (SSA) - an act that
many of you have been instrumental in passing - have not been able to make up for this sharp
decline in General Fund allocations.10 And it is worth reminding that the SSA revenue was never
intended to replace General Fund dollars but instead it was intended to provide additional funding.

The deprioritization of funding for our schools needs to be reversed. The share of the General Fund
that goes to K-12 education needs to return to the level it was in the many years before 2019.
To that end, we support both the path forward laid out by Governor Kotek11 about reviewing the
education budget process and we urge you to formally include educational stakeholders in that
review and we also support updating the Quality Education Model (QEM).
The QEM was developed in 1999. Since then, federal education legislation has changed, state
education legislation has changed. And may we remind you, in 1999 you had to hang up your
phone in order to be able to dial up into the internet. The world has changed and so have the
opportunities and challenges for our children. Our model of quality education in the state should
reflect those changes fully.
In summary, we ask you,
● to support a thorough update to the Quality Education Model to reflect the current
challenges and opportunities we see in our schools;
● to work with the Governor’s office and all educational stakeholders on reviewing the
education budget process;
● and, ultimately, to re-prioritize providing high quality education to our children in
allocations from the General Fund.
Our children deserve it.
Thank you.
