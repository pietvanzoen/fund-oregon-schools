I am writing on behalf of Foundations for a Better Oregon (FBO), a partnership
of five major Oregon philanthropic organizations committed to ensuring
Oregon’s systems support every child to learn, grow, and thrive. We support
Senate Bill 1555 with the -5 amendment, and applaud the Legislature for
acting with urgency to retire Oregon’s broken Quality Education Model
(QEM) and replace it with a modern cost model that aligns with our state’s
K-12 accountability strategies and improvement goals.
Modeling the cost of a high-quality education is not just a financial exercise. It
should clearly connect Oregon’s education investments and costs to a critical
ultimate purpose: improving student outcomes. Unlike the biennial state
budgeting process, which aims to fund current services and is agnostic to
quality goals, a cost model should illuminate the path to a high-quality
public education system by estimating long-term costs in line with
Oregon’s specific goals to improve student outcomes and school quality.
The -5 amendment meets the mark by asking independent nonpartisan
experts to build an accurate and actionable cost model that intentionally
links to Oregon’s new K-12 accountability and improvement law (Senate
Bill 141). This approach will ensure the new cost model follows evidencebased best practices for student learning and success, accounts for different
needs in different communities, and remains laser-focused on reaching
Oregon’s goals to improve education outcomes.
Though the QEM’s retirement is long overdue, we recognize the long line of
Quality Education Commission (QEC) members who volunteered extensive
time and energy to better understand the conditions and costs that drive a

Thank you for your leadership and continued partnership in support of

Senate Bill 1555-5 this session.
