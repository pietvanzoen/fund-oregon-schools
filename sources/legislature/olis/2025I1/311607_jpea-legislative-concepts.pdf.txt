Legislative Concepts for Introduction

LC 43 modifies the process used to determine the amount of state funding required for public K12 education. The legislative concept makes the following changes:
•

It modifies the state’s quality goals for public education to include compliance with the
requirements of a standard school district, licensure of every teacher, and satisfaction of
the statewide targets for the metrics identified in Senate Bill 141 (2025). The measure
also repeals existing language related to educational goals.

•

It moves the responsibility for publishing a report relating to the sufficiency of public
education funding from the Joint Public Education Appropriations Committee (JPEA) to
the Joint Committee on Ways and Means and repeals the statutory requirements related to
JPEA.

•

It moves the responsibility for creating an education cost model from the Quality
Education Commission (QEC) to the Legislative Policy and Research Office via contract
with a public or private entity and requires creation of a new cost model every eight years
instead of each biennium. It also provides for modeling prototype schools that account for
the diversity of educational settings in Oregon. For the biennia in which no new cost
model is created, it requires the Department of Administrative Services to adjust the
dollar amount of the cost model by taking into consideration inflation and other costs.
The measure also repeals statutory requirements related to the QEC.

•

It enacts a requirement that all school districts and education service districts must
comply with the requirements of state and federal law to be considered a standard school
district. To conform with this provision, the measure removes specific requirements in
certain statutes that require compliance in order to be a standard school district.

•

It declares an emergency and takes effect on passage.
