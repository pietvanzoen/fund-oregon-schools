Statement on Today’s Oregon Revenue Forecast
SALEM, OR – The Oregon Office of Economic Analysis released their latest Revenue Forecast.
Senator David Brock Smith (R-Curry, Coos & Douglas Counties) issued the following statement:
“Oregonians pay some of the highest taxes in the nation, yet many feel they aren’t getting a fair return
on their investment in the services they receive,” said Senator David Brock Smith.
“Today’s revenue forecast projects roughly $245 million more for the next budget cycle, but that gain
is driven almost entirely by volatile Wall Street “wealth effects”, capital gains, dividends, and
retirement distributions from higher-income households. These dollars are inflated and not
sustainable for several reasons that include outward migration. Markets have already declined more
than $2 trillion since the November 3 election, and economists themselves note this trend will
eventually reverse. We cannot lock in permanent spending on temporary windfalls.”
“Meanwhile, Oregonians, Main Street businesses and job creators are feeling the real pain. Utility
rates alone have increased 65 percent over the last decade, and Oregon continues to lag nationally in
economic growth with unemployment still above the national average. Oregon’s tax competitiveness
has also slipped significantly in recent years, to near the bottom of the nation.”
“Oregon Democrats need to work with Republicans to right-size government and make life more
affordable for working families. We stand ready to help implement common sense policies that reduce
costs and the tax burden, streamline permitting, retain industry, and grow jobs, so that every
Oregonian can get ahead,” concluded Senator David Brock Smith.
