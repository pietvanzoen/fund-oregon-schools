House Republicans: Oregon Forecast Shows Declining School Enrollment
and Growing Economic Risks
SALEM, Ore. — Oregon's fourth quarter economic and revenue forecast, released this morning by the
Office of Economic Analysis, projects that K-12 enrollment will decline 13 percent over the next decade,
following a decline of 36,800 students over the last ten years.
The forecast also projects population growth averaging just 0.4 percent annually through 2035, entirely
dependent on net migration. Oregon's unemployment rate remains above the national average.
While economists increased projected 2027-29 state resources by $245 million, much of the improvement
is tied to capital gains and investment income rather than wage growth. Economists also told lawmakers
that an equity market correction remains possible.
Today, House Republican Leader Lucetta Elmer (R-McMinnville) released the following statement:
“Thirteen percent fewer students in our schools over the next decade should get everyone's attention.
We're already seeing districts close schools, and this forecast raises serious questions about Oregon's
future.
Yes, the state is projecting a little more revenue, but that doesn't mean Oregon families are doing better.
Much of that increase is coming from investment income, not stronger paychecks.
We cannot build a responsible state budget around a strong stock market and hope it lasts, and we cannot
expect families and businesses to choose Oregon if we continue making it more expensive to live and
work here.
House Republicans want this session focused on affordability, economic opportunity and responsible
spending. Those shouldn't be partisan priorities, and we're ready to work with anyone willing to deliver
results for Oregonians.”
