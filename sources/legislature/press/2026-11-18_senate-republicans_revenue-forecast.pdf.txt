Strong Stock Market Is No Substitute for a Strong Oregon
Economy
SALEM, Ore. – Oregon’s latest quarterly revenue forecast shows an $11 million increase
in General Fund revenue for the current 2025-27 biennium, while raising expectations for
the upcoming 2027-29 biennium by $233 million. Much of the state's projected future
revenue growth is tied to investment income rather than sustained economic growth.
Senate Republican Leader Bruce Starr (R-Dundee) released the following statement:
“Wall Street is doing fine, but that doesn't mean Oregon is. The stock market is propping
up state revenues while Oregon families and businesses are getting hammered by rising
costs. The state can't keep making permanent spending decisions based on stock market
gains that could disappear overnight.
Oregon needs more people working, more businesses investing here, and more families
who can afford to call this state home. Until we start addressing why Oregon is becoming
increasingly expensive to live and do business in, no amount of stock market growth is
going to fix the underlying problems with our economy.”
The forecast also shows that Oregon's economy is trailing the nation, population growth
remains sluggish, and energy costs continue to rise. Oregon's population is expected to
grow by just 0.4% per year through 2035, and K-12 enrollment is projected to drop another
13% over the next decade.
