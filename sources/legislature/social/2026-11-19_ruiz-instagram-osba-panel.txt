I had the opportunity to join an education panel with the Oregon School Boards Association (OSBA) to discuss education policy, school funding, and the challenges facing our school districts.

Through my visits to schools, conversations with school board members and superintendents, and especially my recent visit to James John Elementary, I have heard directly from students, parents, educators, and community members about the realities facing our schools. I have seen firsthand how difficult these decisions are and how financial pressures affect the people and classrooms at the heart of our education system. Those conversations have stayed with me.

During today’s panel, I previewed several pieces of work focused on both immediate relief and long-term solutions.

Our long-term vision is to build a new education funding system that is equitable, targeted, and tailored to the unique needs of each region of Oregon. This includes developing an adequate resource needs cost model that increases the number of prototype school models to better understand what resources students need to succeed.

We also discussed the OSAA three-tier system to protect our student-athletes and an initial proposal to use one-time Education Stability Fund dollars to provide targeted PERS relief to districts experiencing significant financial strain, particularly those that have already tapped into their own reserves.

What I heard at James John Elementary reinforced why I believe this immediate relief approach makes sense. Our districts cannot wait years for a better funding system while making increasingly difficult decisions today. Using one-time dollars responsibly to help stabilize struggling districts, while we develop lasting reforms, is a thoughtful way to address both needs. It won’t fix the long-term issues until we overhaul the whole education funding system. Work is being done in that matter.

We cannot wait for long-term reform to address today’s challenges, and we cannot let today’s challenges prevent us from building a better system for tomorrow.

Thank you to OSBA for creating space for this important conversation. There is much work ahead, and I look forward to continuing it.
