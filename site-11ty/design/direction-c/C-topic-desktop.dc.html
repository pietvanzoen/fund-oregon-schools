The Student Investment Account: what the money funds and what the Oregon Department of Education reports it did

In short

Schools and other grantees spent almost all of this money each year, mostly on staff. The state's reports don't yet show whether it changed results for students.

What we think this means

Our interpretation, not a source

The spending picture is clear and consistent. Grantees reported spending almost all of each year's allocation, mostly on staff. The same three of the law's four allowed uses lead every year: health and safety first, then well-rounded education and smaller classes.

The results picture says less. The department compares each grantee with targets the grantee set with the department, not with what would have happened without the money, so a met target doesn't show the account caused the change; that's our reasoning, not the department's.

What the documents say

  • The Student Investment Account was created by the Student Success Act in 2019. It gives school districts, eligible charter schools and youth corrections and juvenile detention education programs non-competitive grants once they meet the application requirements SIA 2026 Legislative Report, p.3
